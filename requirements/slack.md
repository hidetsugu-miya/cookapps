# Slack requirements

## S-1 Paste a mail into Slack

From the mail detail, the "Slack に貼る" button opens a screen of its own (not a popover) of two panes: on the left the preview of the post, on the right the conversation with Claude of S-4. The left pane has the destination chip and "元の内容に戻す" at the top, the preview in the middle (it shows the text as plain text with its line breaks; pressing it opens the edit field in its place, and leaving the field shows the preview again), and the "投稿" button in a foot at the bottom. The text starts as the subject, sender, date and body of the latest message (the body cut at 3,000 characters), and what is posted, under your own name, is the text as the user edited it (`*` stays bold; blank or past 10,000 characters is refused). There is no separate note. The destination is chosen on the screen with a chip that opens the picker (the workspace, recent destinations, joined channels with a search, or a channel or thread link); none is chosen by itself, and "投稿" is enabled only with a destination and a non-blank text. "元の内容に戻す" (pressed twice) puts the starting text back. The foot of the left pane ("投稿") and the foot of the right pane (the request field of S-4) are one height and fixed to the bottom of the screen, whatever the text or the conversation, and only the parts above them scroll. The screen shows no placeholder and no text that explains a field or a button. A post closes the screen, discards the draft and returns to the mail detail with a notice; a failure shows the error and keeps the text. "メールに戻る" returns to the mail detail (nothing is fetched again, the scroll position is restored), and the draft (text, destination and conversation of S-4) is kept in memory for one mail at a time, keyed by the thread and its latest message, so reopening the same mail finds it and another mail starts anew. You can have several workspaces. Authentication is a User OAuth Token per workspace (scopes `chat:write`, `channels:read`, `groups:read`); a Bot User OAuth Token (`xoxb-`) is refused when it is registered, and one already stored shows the same message in settings

## S-2 Summarize a Slack message into a Todo

Paste the URL of a Slack message into the Todo add field. cookapps fetches the whole thread of the message (`conversations.replies`, 200 messages a page, at most 20 pages, for a link to a reply as well as to the first message; a blank message is skipped except the linked one, a message a later page repeats is read once, a message's lines after the first are indented by two spaces so that nothing in a text can pass as a heading or a sender, and a later page that cannot be read ends the read there; a linked message past the page cap is read on its own, and the replies the cap or that failure left unread count as omitted, at least one unless only the linked reply was left, which was read on its own and counted by the thread's reply count), summarizes it with the Claude Code CLI (`claude -p`, the model and effort of A-2 with sonnet and medium as the default, no tools, no MCP, no settings), and opens the result as a page of the Todo screen, the draft of the Todo. While the summary is on its way, a row above the list says so (and a failed summary is a closable row with the error). A thread whose lines, with their headings, fit 10,000 characters is sent whole in one call. A thread of one message that does not fit with its heading is that message alone, cut to 10,000 characters with its heading and the note below. A longer thread is split: the first message (2,000 characters at most; none when the thread's own message was blank) and the linked message (4,000 characters at most) and the latest replies (whole messages from the newest back, 3,000 characters; a single newer message over that is cut to it) go as they are, and the rest is grouped, from the newest back and at message boundaries, into chunks of at most 10,000 characters (at most 5, the ones nearest the end of the thread; a message over that is cut to it), each summarised by its own `claude -p` call (one at a time, the newest chunk first, with the same model and effort, a fixed Japanese instruction rather than the editable body of S-3, and the key points as the answer, as for M-8). The points reach the final call, at most 3,000 characters and never so many that the input passes 10,000, under "[途中の N 件の要点]" between the first message and the linked one (at the very start when the first message sent is one of the latest replies, so that it never comes between them; what a chunk leaves of its share of the characters goes to the chunks after it); the sections are headed "[スレッドの最初のメッセージ]", "[対象のメッセージ]" (what the task is about) and "[最新の返信]" (the present state), and "[スレッドの返信]" in a thread sent whole (its replies after the linked message show the present state too). When replies were left unread (the end of the thread was not read), the last message sent is followed by one fixed indented line ("（これより新しい返信は読めていません）"), so that the last message read is not taken for the end of the thread (if the line would pass 10,000 characters, that message is cut by the excess first). Every message that is cut, whichever part it is (first, linked, latest or a chunk's), is followed by one fixed indented line ("（このメッセージは長いため、ここで切っています。続きは読めていません）") in the text sent to Claude, and the whole stays within 10,000 characters. The chunk calls together have a time budget of 180 seconds, and only one chunk call runs at a time over all summaries (a slot of the two `claude -p` slots is left for M-8, S-3 and S-4): the wait for that one slot is part of the budget and a chunk that cannot get it before less than 30 seconds of the budget are left fails; no chunk starts with less than 30 seconds of the budget left, and the chunk running is cut off at it (a call never runs longer than the time limit of one call, A-2). The wait for a `claude -p` slot inside a call (60 seconds) is not part of the budget, so a chunk may end up to 60 seconds after it. A chunk that fails or is cut off is logged (never its text), stops the chunks still to run (a stalled network or a refused login would fail them all), and the summary goes on to the final call. The messages of a chunk that failed or did not run count as omitted; a failure of the final call is an error. `omitted` of the summary (the unread replies, the chunks left out and the failed ones; set by Rust only, never read from the model's answer) shows in the draft as "N 件のメッセージは要約に含まれていません" when above 0, and is not sent on to `todoist_add_from_slack`. When the linked message is cut (at 4,000 characters in a split thread, or to fit a thread of one message), `targetCut` of the summary (set by Rust only, never read from the model's answer, and not sent on to `todoist_add_from_slack` either) shows in the draft as "対象のメッセージは長いため、先頭だけを要約しました". The ID and name of every Todoist project (from the last sync) go to Claude with the thread, and it names the one that fits best (only an ID in that list is kept). The draft is a page inside the Todo screen (`#todo-draft`, a section that takes the place of the list, as the task detail does: the side menu stays, there is no dimmed layer, the list of projects beside it is inert so that no other list can be chosen under it, and the focus goes into the page; a refresh of the Todo data neither closes it nor returns to the list; it opens only while the Todo screen is shown on its list, and a summary that is ready while another draft is open, while another screen is shown, or while a task is open, waits as a row "Todo の案ができました" and opens when it can). It is shaped like a task's detail: on the left the task name and the description (a short bullet list of the key points) as fields, and beside them (280px wide) the project, the date and the labels. On the right of them, past a divider the user drags, is the pane for Claude (S-18). The Todo screen's header has the back button of the task detail at its left (`#todo-back`, `aria-label` "戻る", the same part) and the title "Todo の案". The draft's own header row has, at its left, the source workspace (`.draft-team`) and the first line of the linked message (`.draft-quote`, in a lighter color; the message link is the tooltip of both) and, at its right, "Slack で開く" (`a.draft-open`, a link to the message that the app opens in the default browser, as the Slack thread's own does), and no ✕ and no entry to the prompt of S-3. The first line is the linked message's text as plain text (S-10's conversion of Slack's markup, where a mention is the user's name, or the handle of the user group, that the Slack list and thread already hold in memory, so no call to Slack is made for it, and "@不明" when that is not known), every run of white space, line breaks included, as one space, and at most 200 characters; Rust sets it as `targetText` of the summary from the message it has already read for the summary (no new call to Slack, and never from the model's answer), and does not send it on to `todoist_add_from_slack`. The row keeps it on one line and cuts what does not fit with "…" (CSS), so the workspace and "Slack で開く" stay in view; it is drawn as text (`textContent`), and is left out when the message has no text. The project (Claude's recommendation at first, tagged "推奨" on the field and on its row in the project menu, else the current one) and the date can be edited, as can the name and the description: the date field opens the same popover as a task's detail (a text field for Todoist's natural language, and a month to pick a day from, which keeps the time typed in the field (`15:00`, `9:30`, `15時`, `15時30分`, each with 午前 or 午後 in front, alone or after a date, 今日, 明日 or 明後日; an hour of 24 or more or minutes of 60 or more, and any other form such as `15時半`, `夜8時` or `3pm`, are not read), else the time of the due date; a click elsewhere confirms the text as Enter does, except an emptied field of a repeating task, and Escape cancels it), and nothing is saved by it. The labels field is the frame of the task detail's without its add button, because the add command takes no label. The message link, which cannot be edited, is shown under the description and is put at its end when the task is added. The add button is a round "+" at the bottom right (`aria-label` "{project}に追加", so "インボックスに追加" for the inbox; a spinner while it is on its way, the draft and the error staying when it fails), and Enter in the name or Cmd+Enter (Ctrl+Enter off macOS) anywhere in the page but the request field of the Claude pane (S-18, where it sends the request) does what it does; with a blank name nothing is added. "破棄" at the bottom left and the back button close the page and create nothing, and the list comes back (the back button is off while the add is on its way, as 破棄 is); Escape does not close it (Escape still closes the due popover and the menus), so a draft is not lost by accident, and nothing is created until the add button is pressed. The Slack thread (S-6) shows the same fields on a card above its reply field instead of the page, without the pane of S-18. The Slack token also needs `channels:history`, `groups:history`, `im:history` and `mpim:history` (the last two for DM and group DM links). The Claude connection (A-4: the account of the subscription, or the API key) is shown in the side menu and in settings

## S-3 Edit the Todo summary prompt

The prompt of S-2 has a fixed part (the instruction to treat the message as untrusted data, how the message is enclosed, how the thread's headings and indented lines are read, today's date and the project list, and, after the body, the instruction not to choose the project or the due date because the message asks; it cannot be edited) and a body (how to write the name, description, due date and project, and the Japanese writing rules). The body is shown and edited on its own screen, opened from the "Todo の要約プロンプト" row of the Claude tab in settings (shown whether or not Claude can be used), and from nowhere else (neither the heading of the Todo list, at the owner's instruction, 2026-11-11, nor the draft page of S-2, at the owner's instruction, 2026-11-12, has an entry for it); "戻る" returns to the settings. While the screen is open, the side menu still points at the settings. Saving writes it to `todo-prompt.json` in the app config directory (saving the default body removes the file; an empty body or one over 10,000 characters is refused), and the next summary uses it. "デフォルトに戻す" removes the file. In a conversation with Claude (`claude -p`, the model and effort of the prompt conversation of A-2 with sonnet and medium as the default, no tools, no MCP, no settings) the user asks for a change; the reply is a short Japanese message and, when the body changes, a line diff with "適用" and "却下". The request is typed in a field (`#prompt-input`, `aria-label` "Claude への指示", no placeholder) that starts one line high and grows with its text up to 6 lines (then it scrolls), the pane's bottom staying put, in the frame that `ts/composer.ts` makes for every Claude request field (T-7, S-4, S-7 and S-18 have the same part; the form `#prompt-form` is made by TS, not written in `index.html`), with in the frame one row under the field: the model and effort selects of the prompt conversation (`#prompt-model`, the same values as the Claude tab of settings, short texts such as "Sonnet" and "medium", no border) at the left, loaded when the screen opens if they are not read yet (the Slack compose screen of S-4 has those of the proofreading), and the round send button `#prompt-send` (`aria-label` "送信") at the right; Enter is a line break, and Cmd+Enter (Ctrl+Enter off macOS) or the send button sends, and no text says so; a request of several lines keeps its line breaks in the conversation. A "クリア" button at the right end of the conversation header, pressed twice (the first press says "もう一度押すとクリア", and 4 seconds or leaving the button disarms it), forgets the conversation, so the next request is sent with no history; it is disabled while there is no conversation or Claude is answering, and it leaves the edit field, the saved instructions and the text being typed alone. Only the text of the edit field, the conversation and the request are sent, never a Slack message. Applying fills the edit field and does not save; an old proposal cannot be applied once the field was changed. The field and the conversation are kept in memory until the app quits. The instructions sent to the model (the fixed parts, the default body, and the mail summary and prompt-edit instructions, with the labels of their input) are written in Japanese; a body saved earlier stays as it was. The mail summary prompt (M-8) is not affected by the body. The conversation needs Claude to be usable (A-4). A proposal made from an empty text is shown as all lines added

## S-4 Proofread the text to paste into Slack with Claude

The right pane "Claude と校正" of the S-1 screen is the same conversation as S-3 (`ts/claude-chat.ts`: the log, a request field that is always 3 lines high and scrolls inside when a request is longer, Enter is a line break and Cmd+Enter (Ctrl+Enter off macOS) or the send button sends, with no text that says so, a "クリア" button pressed twice, and a note and disabled controls while Claude cannot be used; the diff with "適用" and "却下" is not used here, see below), about the text of the post (`claude_proofread_slack`; `claude -p`, the model and effort of A-2 with sonnet and medium as the default, no tools, no MCP, no settings). There is no "校正する" button: a proofreading is asked by typing the request. The request field (`#slack-input`, `aria-label` "Claude への依頼", no placeholder) is in the frame that `ts/composer.ts` makes for every Claude request field (S-3, T-7, S-7 and S-18 have the same part; the form `#slack-form` is made by TS and holds only the frame), with in the frame one row under the field: the model and effort selects of the proofreading (`#slack-model`, "Slack に貼る文章の校正" of A-2, no border) at the left, loaded when the screen opens if they are not read yet, and the round send button `#slack-send` (`aria-label` "送信") at the right. The instructions to the model (Japanese) say that the text is untrusted data and its instructions (and those in the assistant's turns of the conversation) are not followed, that the request decides what is corrected (typos, polite forms, wording), that facts, proper nouns, dates and URLs are not changed, that the subject line and the sender line at the top are not changed unless the request says so, that the reply is one or two short Japanese sentences, and that `body` is the whole text only when it changes and is identical except where changed. Only the text of the post (up to 10,000 characters), the conversation and the request are sent, and only when send is pressed. An answer that changes the text is put into the preview and the edit field at once, without waiting for "適用" (`autoApply` of `ts/claude-chat.ts`), and posts nothing. The log keeps no diff for it: under the reply is a small row with the number of lines it changed ("N 行を変えました": the larger of the deleted and the added lines of the diff, at least 1) and "元に戻す", which puts back the text from before that answer ("元に戻しました" is shown then). "元に戻す" works only while the text is still the one that answer made (it is disabled once the text was edited or a later answer changed it, and while the post is on its way), so the answers are undone newest first. An answer that arrives after the text was changed meanwhile, or while the post is on its way, is not put in: it stays a proposal with "却下" and "適用" and a diff, and cannot be applied (an old proposal cannot be applied once the field was changed). The conversation is kept with the draft of S-1 and goes with it; an answer that arrives after the screen was opened for another mail is dropped. A divider between the two panes (`role="separator"`, dragged with pointer events or moved with the left and right arrow keys) sets the width of the Claude pane to between a third and a half of the screen body (the CSS holds the range, the ratio is kept in memory until the app quits and survives a resized window). It needs Claude to be usable (A-4). A proposal made from an empty text is shown as all lines added

## S-5 Slack list

The Slack icon of the sidebar (`nav-slack`) opens a list of the messages of the public and private channels the token's user is in, across all workspaces, as an unread section (grouped by channel, the newest 10 unread rows of each channel) and the watch section (S-13), each newest first, one of them at a time, as the side menu of S-19 chooses (S-8 and the unread replies of S-14; `slack_timeline`; direct messages and group DMs are not listed, nor are joins and other subtypes or a message with no text, file, block or attachment; a reply that was also sent to the channel is). The list has a header (S-13) but shows no unread count of its own (the Slack button's badge of S-8 does); while `pendingChannels` of the answer (channels no read has reached yet, over all workspaces) is above 0, the muted "残り N チャンネルを読み込み中" (`#slack-pending`, 32px from the left like the list) is shown above the notice, and takes no room while hidden. The feed starts 8px below the top, as the inbox does, and the notice has 8px above it. A row holds links and a toggle, so it is a container with one real button stretched over it (`.feed-row` holds `button.feed-open`, the only key stop of the row besides those, with the focus and scroll position kept across a redraw; its `aria-label` is the name, `#channel` and the head of the text, and it shows no tooltip). What is drawn lies above the button and lets the press through (`pointer-events: none`), except links, the toggle of S-14 and the "N 件の返信" of S-8, so pressing anywhere else opens the thread, and a link opens in the default browser (the navigation hook) without opening the thread or marking anything read; no text of the list can be selected. A row of the watch section has the sender's icon (the initial on a color taken from the name until the icon is here or when it cannot be fetched) with a dot in the workspace's color (the color of the workspace's avatar in the sidebar), the name, `#channel`, the body, "返信 N 件" when above 0, and the date as the inbox writes it. A row of the unread section is in the group of its channel (S-8): the icon with no dot, the name, the time ("9:05"), the body, and the button "N 件の返信" when above 0. The body is what the thread draws of the message (S-11, S-12: `rich`, `blocks`, `showText` and `attachments` of `TimelineMessage`, made for the rows listed only, in full and cached reads, by the same functions as `thread_message`; `text` stays the plain text): the fields in two columns, buttons as `a.text-btn` links, and the styles. A body of paragraphs alone is two lines at most; one with blocks, attachments, a code block or a quote is about 12 lines at most, the rest hidden, so that one long post of a bot cannot take the screen. No image is fetched and there is no "画像を表示" (an image with an address is left out, one without draws its description), and a file has no thumbnail: a message of files only says "（添付ファイル）". The unread mark and the unread section are S-8 and the watch section is S-13; there is no mark for a Todo made, and no hover action but the bookmark of S-13. One read asks `conversations.history` for at most 40 channels of a workspace (not the ones kept off the list, S-9): the channels never read first, then the ones read longest ago; the others show the messages their last read kept in memory (`TimelineCache`), so a workspace of more channels is covered over several reads and the answer takes a bounded time. A rate limit (429) is not retried in this read: it ends that workspace's read for the round and stops its reads until Slack's `Retry-After` has passed (60 seconds when it gives none, 10 minutes at most), while the others are read and the kept messages are still listed. A channel never read that fails stays pending and is tried in the next round. The list is read at startup (`slack_load` first; with no workspace nothing is called) and again every `refreshSecs` of the answer (120 seconds, or 300 while every workspace is connected by Socket Mode, S-16; 60 seconds while a channel is unread, a workspace has over 40 channels, a watched thread is not checked yet, S-13, or a thread of S-14 was never checked) while the window is visible (nothing is read while it is hidden, and it is read at once on the return when the list held is that old), on whichever screen is shown, by the one read of `ts/slack-list.ts`, so that whether the Slack screen is shown does not double it and only the latest read is used (the badge of S-8 follows it). Showing the Slack screen reads only when no read is on its way and the list held was read `refreshSecs` of its answer ago or longer (otherwise it is drawn as it is; a workspace added or removed in between, in settings or by `slack_load`, makes it stale at once, and when the last one is gone the rows, the failure and the badge are dropped; a failed `slack_load` keeps the workspaces known, so the tray and the refresh stay). A read while another screen is shown draws nothing of the list (it is drawn from what is held when the screen is shown), never calls `show()` and never switches the screen. An answer replaces the rows in place (the scroll position and the focused row stay, `show()` is not called). A workspace that could not be read, and the scopes a token lacks (`users:read`, `files:read`, `channels:write` and `groups:write` are optional), show as a notice above the list with "設定を開く" (the Slack tab) while the rows that were read stay (a workspace that could not be read still lists the messages its earlier reads kept, and a read that fails altogether keeps the rows and shows its error in the notice). The first read shows a spinner; with no workspace the list says so with "設定を開く"; a failed read with nothing to show (the call failed, or every connected workspace failed and there is no message) says so with "再読み込み"; when only some workspaces failed, the notice above the list says which and the empty list says "メッセージはありません" (also when the call failed while a list with no message is held). Messages, names and icons are in memory only. Sender icons and the user's own icons are fetched by `fetch_images` (no token) as data: URIs, so the CSP stays; Rust gives an icon URL only when it is https and its host is `slack-edge.com` or a subdomain of it or `secure.gravatar.com` (a bot's, a user's; any other host gives no icon and the initial shows). A read asks `users.info` about at most 40 users, chosen after the ones the cache answers (an hour; five minutes for one Slack could not describe) are left out, and once a call finds `users:read` missing, or the scopes remembered for the workspace say so, the rest of that call's lookups are skipped; each workspace's own user ID (`auth.test`) is asked once and remembered in memory until the workspace is removed. A mention of a user group (`<!subteam^S…>` in the text, a `usergroup` element in `rich_text`) is drawn as `@handle`, the handle that `usergroups.list` (with `include_disabled`, so that a group that was disabled is named too) gives for its ID, and as `@不明` while the handle is not known; a mention with a label (`<!subteam^S…|@handle>`) is drawn with the label as before. The list of a workspace is asked once after its read, when the cache has none or the answer is old (an hour; five minutes for a call that failed), and is kept in memory (`TimelineCache`) until the workspace is removed; it is not asked for the cached read (S-9) or the read by events (S-16), which use what is kept. A workspace whose token lacks `usergroups:read`, known from the scopes remembered for it, is not asked; a call that finds it missing is kept as a miss for five minutes, and neither is shown as a failure or a missing scope in the notice. The sender names and icons need `users:read`, the names of user groups `usergroups:read`, the attached images `files:read` (settings shows what a token lacks of `users:read` and `files:read` without calling the workspace short of permission, and the setup hint lists the scopes, `usergroups:read` last, and its sentence on the optional scopes names `users:read`, `files:read` and `usergroups:read` as what gives the names and icons, the attached images and the names of user groups; `usergroups:read` is optional and is not listed as lacking, so it is not among the scopes of the status line in settings)

## S-6 Slack thread

A row opens the thread in place of the list, filling the content area, with a back button that returns to the list at the scroll position it had (or, when the side menu has a channel chosen, to the history of S-20 at the scroll position it had) and reads nothing more than the normal refresh (the list keeps being read in the background and the thread stays). The tinted header has the back button, `#channel` with the workspace name muted, "Slack で開く" (a link to the permalink, which the default browser opens through Rust's navigation hook) and "Todo にする". The messages (`slack_thread` with the workspace, the channel and the thread's own timestamp, `threadTs ?? ts`) are drawn oldest first: a 40px avatar, the name, the time and the text as text (only URLs are linked, by `linkify`, and the styles of S-12 are drawn; a labeled Slack link shows as `label (URL)` when the label is not the URL), and the blocks and attachments of S-11 where the message has them, the reactions of S-22 under a message that has any, and after the first message the separator "返信 N 件" when there are replies. Attached images show as thumbnails under the text: a placeholder until `slack_file_images` answers (the token goes to `files.slack.com` only, in Rust; the images are kept in memory per workspace in a cache of their own in `SlackState`, which `fetch_images` cannot read and removing the workspace clears), then the image linked to the file in Slack; a file that is not an image, and an image that could not be fetched, show as a chip with the file name that opens the file in Slack. A thread that cannot be read shows the error in the pane with "再読み込み". A thread opened from the list (another thread than the one shown last) opens at its newest message: the messages are scrolled to the end, and a thread that fits the pane looks as it did. While the user has not scrolled up from the end, they stay at the end when an image changes their height (a thumbnail replacing its placeholder, and an image of S-11 once it has loaded), as the Orca timeline of A-7 does: within 40px of the end counts as the end, and once the user scrolls up from it nothing moves the messages until they come back within 40px. Opening the same thread again, and "再読み込み", keep the scroll position of a thread already drawn; a thread whose first read failed opens at its newest message when a read works, and so does a thread that was left before its read ended. "Todo にする" (shown only while Claude can be used and Todoist has a token) summarises the message by its permalink (`todoist_summarize_slack`, the same as S-2) and shows a card above the reply field with the fields of the draft of S-2 (the name and the description, the due date and the project, the source, the notes of `omitted` and `targetCut`, edited as there; no labels), without the link to the prompt screen; "追加" calls `todoist_add_from_slack` and shows "Todo に追加しました", "破棄" closes the card. The reply field (placeholder "スレッドに返信", three lines high, growing to six; Enter is a line break, Cmd+Enter (Ctrl+Enter off macOS) or "返信する" posts, which is off while the field is blank) posts with `slack_reply` (the checks and the errors of S-1; the recent destinations of S-1 are not touched; its optional `markdown` is true when the text is Markdown, converted as S-21 says, and the screen does not pass it yet). On success the field empties, the thread is read again and scrolled to the end, and "スレッドに返信しました" shows; on a failure the error shows on the thread and the text stays; the field and the button wait while the post is on its way. When the read stopped before the end of the thread (`truncated`), "これより新しい返信は読めていません" ends the messages. Opening the same thread again keeps its Todo card, and a reply or a summary that ends after the thread was left and opened again is applied to it. The reply and the conversation of S-7 are kept in memory per thread (the workspace, the channel and the thread's timestamp) until the app quits

## S-7 Ask Claude about the thread

## S-12 Slack styles in the thread

The thread of S-6 draws the styles of Slack's mrkdwn in the text of a message: `*bold*` (`<strong>`), `_italic_` (`<em>`), `~strike~` (`<s>`), `` `code` `` (`<code>`), a block between ``` marks (`<pre><code>`, scrolling sideways on a long line) and lines that start with `&gt;` (`<blockquote>`). A row of the list (S-5) draws them too (`TimelineMessage.rich`, by the same functions); what is sent to Claude (S-2, S-7) stays on plain text. The screen of the thread draws `ThreadMessage.rich`; `ThreadMessage.text` stays as a string that is not read for styles. `slack_mrkdwn.rs` reads the raw text of Slack (not the output of `plain_text`, in which the range of a `<…>` and a `&gt;` are lost) into `ThreadMessage.rich` (`RichBlock`: `paragraph` and `quote` hold `Span`s with `bold`, `italic`, `strike` and `code`, `code` holds a text; ts-rs types), and `ts/slack-rich.ts` only builds the nodes (`textContent` and `linkify`, never `innerHTML`; the elements are made with the DOM API, so an HTML tag in a text stays a text). Order: the code blocks are cut out first (the line break that sets a block off from the text beside it belongs to neither; a ``` with no partner is text; a block of blanks is dropped); the rest goes by lines, a run of lines that start with `&gt;` (and one space) is one quote and the other lines one paragraph, with the line breaks kept in the text and drawn by `white-space: pre-wrap`; in a line, an inline code (closed on the line, not empty), a `<…>` token and a web address are indivisible, and the marks are read around them. A mark works as Slack's does, at the edge of a word and on one line: it opens when it is at the start of the line or after white space or any character but an ASCII letter or digit and the next character is not white space, and it closes at the nearest mark of its kind that follows a non-space and is followed by the end, white space or any character but an ASCII letter or digit, so `snake_case` and `2*3*4` are not styled, a mark with no partner is text, and styles nest (`*_x_*`). A Japanese letter beside a mark does not stop it (not checked against Slack, which the owner cannot compare with; the sample messages are Japanese). A mark at the end of a web address (and the punctuation after it) is not part of the address (`*https://a.example*` is a bold link), and a mark inside one is text. The text of every piece goes through the same functions as `plain_text` (`unescape`, `emojify` of S-10, `token_text`), so the mentions, links and emoji are as before. Inside a code (inline or block) no mark and no emoji is read; `&lt; &gt; &amp;` are undone and a `<…>` is read by `token_text` as in `plain_text`. A `linkify` link is made in every style but not in a code. Which texts are read: the `text` of a message (not when it was posted with `mrkdwn: false`), the `mrkdwn` objects of a section (text and fields) and of a context, and, in an attachment, `pretext`, `text` and the `value` of the fields only when `mrkdwn_in` names `text`, `pretext` or `fields` (Slack's rule). The texts of type `plain_text`, the header, the buttons, the title, footer and author of an attachment, the `rich_text` blocks (flattened, with their quote mark as text) and the `markdown` block are not read, and their marks stay. `>>>` (a quote to the end of the message; a line that starts with it is text) and the contents of an attached `.md` file are not read. The blank and spacing of the text are as before. The colors are the variables of `:root` (`--code`, `--code-line`, `--chip-line`)

Opening a watched row also sets the channel's read position (`slack_mark_read`, with `moves_forward` and the write scopes as S-8 says) to `WatchMark.unreadTo`, the newest unread message the cache holds of the thread (its own message, or a reply also sent to the channel; `thread_unread_to`), so a reply sent to the channel is read with the thread; the badge (`unreadTotal`) is defined as before. **Data**: `slack-watched.json` in the app config directory holds `{"watched":[{"teamId","channel","ts","seenTs"}]}` (IDs and timestamps only, written through a temporary file and a rename as the other lists are; a file that cannot be read, an ID or a timestamp that is not valid counts as not there, and a `seenTs` that is not valid becomes the thread's `ts`); the text, names and icons are in memory only (`TimelineCache.watch`) and are read again after a start. There is no upper limit on the watched threads; each full read of `slack_timeline` checks at most `WATCH_BUDGET` (20) of them over all workspaces with `conversations.replies` (the first page of 200: the thread's own message, then the replies), the ones never checked first and then the ones checked longest ago (`choose_watch_checks`), `HISTORY_CONCURRENCY` calls at a time for each workspace, in the same read (no screen or timer of its own). A 429 is not retried: it ends that workspace's checks for the round and stops them until `Retry-After` has passed (`replies_limited_until`). The checks run beside the read of the read positions (`tokio::join!`), after the user's own ID of each workspace is known. `slack_watch` is stopped by the same wait (it answers an error at once while it lasts, calls `conversations.replies` without a retry, and starts the wait on a 429). A check that fails keeps what the last good one found. A thread Slack says cannot be read (`thread_not_found`, `channel_not_found`, `not_in_channel`, `message_not_found`, or an answer without the thread's own message) is kept as a row that says so (`watch.status` "unreadable": the text "このスレッドは読めません…", the row not pressable) with its bookmark, so it can be let go; a thread not checked yet (or whose checks failed so far) is "pending" ("読み込んでいます…"), and while any is pending `refreshSecs` is 60 (`REFRESH_BUSY_SECS`, `unchecked_watch_rows`), as it is while channels are unread or a thread of S-14 was never checked, and 120 otherwise (300 while every workspace is connected by Socket Mode, S-16). Only the workspaces that could be read in the round are checked. `slack_watch` takes the file lock first and then reads the workspace list again, and saves nothing (and answers `NO_WORKSPACE`) for a workspace that has been removed. `slack_watch_seen` returns at once, without the lock, for a thread the cache has no check of; the screen reads the list again from the cache (`cached: true`) after it when a read was on its way, as `slack_mark_read` does. `WatchMark {newReplies, uncounted, status, unreadTo}` is the `watch` of a `TimelineMessage` of the section (null in the unread section).

## S-14 Unread replies in the Slack list

A thread with unread replies is listed in the unread section of S-8, in the group of its channel, with its replies under its row, also when the thread's own message is read. **What is unread**: Slack's documented read state of a thread is on the parent in `conversations.replies` (`subscribed`, `last_read`); `conversations.history` does not document it, so it is read only from there, and a field that is missing gives no unread reply (not checked on a real workspace). Slack has no public API to write a thread's read position (`conversations.mark` is the channel's), so **reading a thread here does not clear it in Slack**: the position used is the later of Slack's `last_read` and the one kept here. A reply is unread when the parent says `subscribed: true` with a valid `last_read`, its `ts` is later than the position (compared as numbers), it is no reply of the user's own (with the user's own ID not known, the thread is not checked in that read) and it is no reply also sent to the channel (that is a message of the channel, S-8, listed and counted there). Watched threads (S-13) are out of this entirely: no call, no toggle and no count of it, their rows keep "新しい返信 N 件". So are the channels kept off the list (S-9) and a channel the user is no longer in. **Reading**: a full read of `slack_timeline` checks at most `THREAD_BUDGET` (20) threads over all workspaces with `conversations.replies` (the first page of 200, as S-13), `HISTORY_CONCURRENCY` calls at a time for each workspace, beside the read positions and the watched threads (`tokio::join!`). The candidates are the messages held that are the parent of a thread with `reply_count` above 0 (not a reply also sent to the channel), and the threads whose last check found unread replies, even when the parent has left the 20 messages held of its channel. The pure `choose_thread_checks` takes the ones never checked, then the ones whose reply count in `conversations.history` changed since the last check, then the ones with unread replies, then the rest, which only once `THREAD_RECHECK` (1 hour) has passed since their last check, so that threads with no activity do not use the budget in every read; within each, the ones checked longest ago. Only the workspaces that could be read in the round are checked. A 429 is not retried, ends that workspace's checks for the round, and shares `replies_limited_until` with S-13. A thread Slack says cannot be read (`unreadable_code`, or an answer without its own message) loses what was found; another failure keeps it. `refreshSecs` is 60 while a candidate was never checked, so that the first pass drains. `TimelineCache.threads` keeps, in memory only, when each thread was checked, its reply count, the parent, Slack's position, the `ts` of its unread replies and the newest `UNREPLIED_MAX` (20) of them to draw, the newest reply and whether the page was the end of the thread. An entry goes with its workspace, and when its thread is neither held nor unread. A read from the cache (`cached: true`) makes the same answer from these with no call. A thread longer than the page whose newest reply is after the position has unread replies that cannot be counted (`uncounted`, as S-13). **The position kept here**: `slack-thread-seen.json` in the app config directory holds `{"seen":[{"teamId","channel","ts","seenTs"}]}` (IDs and timestamps only, written through a temporary file and a rename as the other lists are; a file that cannot be read, or an entry with an ID or a timestamp that is not valid, counts as not there when the list is read for the screen, but a write (`save_seen_moves`) never overwrites a file that cannot be read and answers an error, as for `slack-watched.json`), because otherwise every start would bring back replies read here. It keeps the 500 with the newest `seenTs`, `seenTs` only moves forward, and removing the workspace removes its entries. `slack_thread_seen` (`teamId`, `channel`, `ts`, `latestTs`) moves it, asks Slack nothing, checks the IDs and timestamps, does nothing for a watched thread or a workspace that is gone, and writes only when the position moved; it is called when a row with unread replies is pressed (with `latestTs`, the newest unread reply) and each time `slack_thread` answers with replies (S-6, with the newest reply read), and the list is made again from the cache when it moved. Letting a thread go (S-13) moves the position to the reply it had been seen up to. **Answer**: `TimelineMessage.unreadReplies` is `{count, uncounted, share, latestTs, replies}` (null in the watch section and when there are none; `count` is 0 when `uncounted`; `share` is what the thread adds to the badge, 1 when `uncounted` else `count`, set by Rust only, and the screen sums it when it takes rows off the badge), and `TimelineReply` has `ts`, `name`, `iconUrl`, `text`, `rich`, `blocks`, `showText`, `attachments` and `hasFiles`. The unread section is sorted by the newest of the row's own unread `ts` and its unread replies, newest first, and keeps the newest 10 rows of each channel (`merge_thread_rows`; the screen draws a group oldest first, S-8); when the parent has left the messages held, the parent saved at the check draws the row. `.unread` (bold) stays the parent's own. `unreadTotal` adds the unread replies of all the threads known, not only the ones listed (a thread that cannot be counted counts 1). `UnreadChannel.threads` of S-8 lists the threads of each channel with unread replies, `ts` and the newest unread reply `latestTs`, also those whose row is past the 10 of the channel. **Screen**: under the row a toggle (`.feed-thread-toggle`, "未読の返信 N 件", "未読の返信あり" when it cannot be counted, `aria-expanded`, a chevron) opens and closes the replies and does nothing else: no read, no thread, no change of the scroll position. It starts open. A closed thread is remembered in memory per thread through the redraws and the return from a thread, and forgotten when the thread has no more unread replies. Open, the replies come oldest first, indented under the row, each with a small avatar, the name, the time and the body drawn as a row's is (the same height rules, S-5), and, when not all are there (it cannot be counted, or above 20), the line "ほかの返信はスレッドで見られます". The replies are part of the row: pressing one opens the thread, and a link in one works. Pressing the row (or the check of a popup, S-17) does what S-8 says, and also `slack_thread_seen`: the row leaves the unread section at once (one still unread itself, when the token cannot write the position, stays without its replies) and the badge drops by its replies. The "既読にする" of a channel (S-15) can also be pressed for unread replies alone: it calls `slack_thread_seen` for every thread in `UnreadChannel.threads`, which is local (it needs no scope and Slack has no say in it), and for a workspace that lacks the write scopes only the replies clear. Hiding a channel and watching a thread take its unread replies out of the badge at once

## S-16 Update the Slack list by Socket Mode events

The owner registers an App-Level Token (`xapp-`, scope `connections:write`) for a workspace, and the app keeps a Socket Mode connection to Slack for it, so that a message posted, changed or deleted reaches the Slack list (S-5, S-8, S-13, S-14) and the Slack button's badge within about a second, instead of at the next read. The reads of the list stay: they bring the channels and threads no event names, and what an event cannot carry.

**Registering.** The Slack tab of the settings has a card "リアルタイム更新" (after the card of S-9; its header has the `?` hint, `#slack-socket-help`), with a row per workspace (avatar, name, a line, the state label with its dot, and when a token is registered an `.icon-btn` "{workspace} の App-Level Token を削除" that asks first, as the unlink of a workspace does), and an add row: a select of the workspaces (`#slack-socket-team`), a password field (`#slack-socket-token`, "App-Level Token（xapp-…）") and "登録". `slack_app_token_set(teamId, token)` trims the token and refuses one that is not `xapp-` followed by letters, digits and hyphens ("App-Level Token は xapp- で始まります"), or for a workspace that is not connected; it accepts the token only when Slack answers `apps.connections.open` with it (a refusal is shown as the other Slack errors are), keeps it in the OS keychain (service `jp.mysis.cookapps.slack-app`, user name the workspace's `team_id`, a value never shown, logged or returned), and starts the connection. The field is emptied as soon as it is read, and an error is shown in `#error`. `slack_app_token_remove(teamId)` stops the connection and deletes the token. Removing a workspace (the unlink button of its row in the Slack card, `slack_remove_workspace`) does both. Both answer the connections (`SocketStatus`: `teamId` and `state`), and `slack_socket_status` answers them at any time.

**State.** The state label of a row is "未登録" (no token; gray), "接続中" (the first connection is on its way; gray), "つながっている" (Slack said hello; green) or "つなぎ直し中" (the connection ended, or could not be made, and the next try is waiting; orange). The line under the name says how often the list is read: "未登録の間は、2 分ごとに読み直します" and "つながるまでは、2 分ごとに読み直します" while not connected, and "新着をすぐに一覧へ出します。読み直しは 5 分ごとです" while connected. Rust sends each change as the event `slack-socket-status` (one `SocketStatus`), and the card follows it.

**Connection.** At startup the app connects every workspace that has a token (`slack::socket::start_all`, spawned in `setup`), and registering a token connects at once. A task per workspace calls `apps.connections.open` with the token, connects to the `wss` address of slack.com it answers (anything else is refused), and answers every envelope with `{"envelope_id": …}` whatever it carries. `hello` makes the state "つながっている". A `disconnect` with the reason `warning` is ignored; `refresh_requested` makes the connection again at once; any other reason, a close, an error, and a socket that sent no frame for 90 seconds (the app pings every 30) make it again after a wait that doubles from 1 second up to 60 (the waits start over when a connection has lasted 60 seconds after its hello, not at the hello itself, so that a connection that drops at once is not made again every second and does not start a full read every second). When Slack refuses the token (`invalid_auth`, `token_revoked` and the other codes of a dead token) the token is deleted (only if the token kept is still the one Slack refused; a token registered meanwhile stays) and the state is "未登録", with the reason logged. TLS is rustls with the system's roots.

**Events.** Only `events_api` envelopes of the workspace (`team_id` of the payload) whose event is a `message` with `channel_type` `channel` or `group`, or with no `channel_type` (the example of `message_deleted` in Slack's reference has none, and `message_changed` is treated alike), are taken, whatever the subtype (a post, a reply, `message_changed`, `message_deleted`); an event with another `channel_type` (`im`, `mpim`) and the other events are answered and ignored. Whether the channel of an event with no `channel_type` is one the user is in is decided by the channel list the app holds, as for the others (below). An event names its channel and, for a reply or a changed or deleted message of a thread, the thread (`thread_ts` of the event, or of its `message` or `previous_message`). It builds no row. The events of a channel that come within one second of the first are one read again (a channel at a time, in order; the threads they name, the newest five at most, are read together). A channel is read again by events at most once in 10 seconds, and a workspace gets at most 20 calls (`conversations.history`, and `conversations.replies` for each thread checked) in a minute for these reads, so that a busy channel cannot use up Slack's limit (about 50 a minute for each method) that the full read needs (up to 40 `conversations.history` calls); a read that is held back is not dropped: it waits, merged with the events that come meanwhile (their threads added), and is made when the gap or the minute allows. A read that would make no call (below) is dropped before the budget, and uses none of it. That read leaves alone a channel kept off the list (S-9), a channel not in the channel list the app holds, and a workspace Slack has rate-limited; it reads the channel's `conversations.history` as the list does (S-5), asks `conversations.info` for its read position only when none is held, checks the named threads with `conversations.replies` as S-13 (a watched thread) and S-14 (a thread with replies held) do, asks `users.info` for the people the new messages draw that the cache does not know, replaces what the cache holds for the channel (`SlackState`), and sends the event `slack-timeline-dirty`. The screen then calls `slack_timeline` with `cached: true` (no call to Slack) and draws it, and sets the badge (`unreadTotal`) from the answer. The rules of S-8 (unread by the channel's read position), S-9, S-13 and S-14 are those of the full read, because the same functions make the list.

**Full reads and the wait.** When a connection is made (the hello), Rust sends `slack-socket-resync` and the screen reads the list in full, as at startup; when a read or a save of S-9, S-13 or S-15 is on its way, it does so once that is done (an event is not sent again after about 6 minutes, so what came while there was no connection must be read). A full read does not overwrite a channel that an event read after the full read began (`TimelineCache::replace_history` keeps those entries of the channels in the list), so that a message posted while it goes on stays in the list and the badge. The list is also made again from the cache at each `slack-socket-status`, so that the wait follows the state. `refreshSecs` is 300 instead of 120 while every workspace is connected (`live_refresh_secs`); it stays 60 in the cases of S-5, S-13 and S-14, and it is 120 as before while any workspace is not connected. The reads on a timer are what shows a channel read on another device: the read position (`channel_marked` and `group_marked`) is an event of the RTM API only, and the Events API does not send it.

**Not in this requirement.** Direct messages and group direct messages (S-5), reactions, files and the other events, and the app's own use of Socket Mode's other payloads (slash commands, interactive components).

The top holds two buttons (`.slack-side-mode`, `aria-pressed` true for the one chosen): "未読メッセージ", with the mail icon and the number of unread messages (`unreadTotal`, the number of the Slack button's badge, which the screen lowers at once as S-8 says; a red pill, "99+" from 100, none for 0), and "ウォッチ" with the bookmark. The one pressed makes the body show its section: the unread rows (S-8, with the unread replies of S-14) or the watch section (S-13), one at a time and each as it was drawn before. The first is chosen at the start, and the choice stays (in memory only) through a redraw, a thread and another screen. Pressing a button while a thread is shown shows the list, as the back button does; a section that is chosen anew starts at the top. A line follows the two buttons when there is a channel to list.

Under it, for each workspace in the order of the sidebar's tray, a heading (a turn mark, a dot in the workspace's color and its name) and the channels the user has joined, public and private (direct messages are not listed, as in S-5), by name. `slack_timeline` gives them as `side` (`SideWorkspace`: `teamId`, `team`, `channels` of `SideChannel`: `id`, `name`, `private`, `unread`), in a full read and in a read from the cache (`cached: true`) alike, from the channel lists the app already holds in memory (`ChannelCache`, `Channel.private` is `is_private` of `users.conversations`), so it asks Slack for nothing more; a workspace whose list is not held yet has no entry, and one with no channel to show (none joined, or every one kept off the list) has no heading. The channels kept off the list (S-9) are not in it. The order is by name with letters of either case together, then by ID (`side_channels`). `unread` is the channel's unread messages among those held and the share of its threads with unread replies (`unread_by_channel`, counted as `unreadTotal` counts them, by the same `is_unread`), so a channel with no known read position has 0. A channel shows `#` or, when it is private, a lock, its name (cut with an ellipsis) and, above 0, its unread number as a red pill (hidden while the channel is hovered or has the focus); the name is bold when the channel has an unread message. The screen lowers the number at once for what it moves to read (a row opened, "既読にする", the check of a card, a thread watched), and the next read gives the exact one. A channel is a button (`.slack-side-btn`) whose press shows its history in the body (S-20), also when a thread is shown, and reads the channel as S-15 says (the unread messages and the unread replies of the channel go at once, and the number in the menu and the badge fall); `aria-pressed` is true for the channel whose history is shown and false for the others, and while it is true neither of the two section buttons is pressed. A section pressed while a history is shown shows the list in that section.

While a channel is hovered or has the focus, a button (`.icon-btn.slack-side-hide`, a circle with a minus, not an eye; `aria-label` "#channel を一覧に出さない") shows at its right end, as a sibling of the channel button (a button is not put in a button) and `visibility: hidden` otherwise. Pressing it does what S-9 says of the button of a row (and, when the history of that channel is shown, the list is shown in its place): the channel goes out of the menu and its rows out of the list at once, `slack_hide_channel` (`teamId`, `channel`) saves it, the notice "#channel を一覧に出さないようにしました" with "元に戻す" (`slack_unhide_channel`) shows, the list is made again from Rust's cache, and a failed save shows the error and brings the channel back the same way.

The heading of a workspace is a button (`.slack-side-group`, `aria-expanded` true while the workspace's channels are all shown) whose mark (a chevron, pointing down while it is open and to the right while it is folded) shows the direction. Pressing it folds the workspace, and pressing it again opens it. A folded workspace keeps its heading and the channels with unread messages (`unread` above 0), and hides the others; the channel whose history is shown (S-20, `aria-pressed` true) stays too, so the pressed button never disappears. The fold is held for each workspace (`teamId`) and saved in `slack-side.json` of the app config directory (`{"folded":["T…"]}`, team IDs only, written through a temporary file and a rename as the other lists are): the screen reads it at the start (`slack_side_folded`; a file that is missing, cannot be read or is broken folds nothing, and an ID that is not valid or is repeated counts as not there) and, at each press, saves the whole set of the folded workspaces (`slack_set_side_folded` with `teamIds`; an ID that is not valid is refused). A failed save shows its error and leaves the fold as pressed. The fold stays through a redraw, a read of the timeline, a thread, another screen and a restart. The focus stays on the heading after the press.

The menu is drawn again only when what it shows changes (the folded workspaces included); its scroll position and the focus (the button that is focused is shown till it loses the focus) stay. The unit tests (`cargo test`) cover the channels of the menu (the order by name with the case together, the hidden ones left out, `private`, the unread numbers) and the count by channel (the messages of the workspace, a message that is read, another workspace's, a thread that cannot be counted counting 1) and `slack-side.json` (written and read back, a missing file and a broken one folding none, the IDs that are not valid and the repeated ones left out). The E2E spec `slack-list.e2e.ts` covers the menu (the buttons and the number, the channels by workspace in order with the lock and the bold ones, the sections chosen and the choice kept, the menu on a thread and a button pressed there, the hide button and the call it makes, the channel that goes from the menu, the failed save, the heading of a workspace as a button with `aria-expanded` that folds it to the unread channels and opens it again, one workspace folded alone and kept through a redraw, the channel whose history is shown kept in a folded workspace, the focus kept on the heading, the read of a channel at its press (the position and the threads with unread replies, none for a workspace whose token cannot write the position, none for a channel with nothing unread), the whole set sent to `slack_set_side_folded` at each press and the error of a failed save, the folds read at the start drawn) with the tests of the rows that the chosen section changed.

## S-20 Slack channel history

Pressing a channel of the side menu (S-19) shows its history in the body, in place of the list or of a thread, and reads the channel as S-15 says (the history itself sets no position: the read is that of the press) (`#slack-channel`, one of the three views of `#slack-pane`, with the font of the Slack screen, A-20). The channel is the one pressed in the menu (`aria-pressed`), and the two section buttons are not. Pressing the same channel again reads the history once more and keeps the scroll position. Pressing a section button, or keeping the channel shown off the list (S-9), shows the list in its place, and the history is forgotten. The choice stays (in memory only) through a thread and another screen, as the section of S-19 does.

The header (`#sc-head`, the tint of the thread header) holds only the channel, a `#` or, when it is private, a lock, and its name, and the name of the workspace muted beside it (`#sc-ic`, `#sc-name`, `#sc-team`). It has no tab (no messages, canvas or files), no button and no count. Under the history is the field of S-21 for posting to the channel.

The history is the newest 50 messages of the channel (`slack_channel_history` with `teamId` and `channel`; one page of `conversations.history`, which the timeline reads already, so no new Slack method and no new scope is used, and no older page is asked for), oldest first from the top, under a separator for each day (`formatDay`, as in S-8). Rust checks that `teamId` is the ID of a connected workspace and `channel` has the form of a channel ID, that the channel is one the workspace's channel list holds (the list the side menu is made of), and the same listing as the timeline (`is_listed`: a person's or a bot's post with text, a file, blocks or attachments; a reply also sent to the channel is in it, joins and other subtypes are not), and answers a message as a row of the list is drawn (`TimelineMessage`, with the names of its senders and their icons, the blocks, attachments, emoji and styles of S-10 to S-12; `unread` is false for each message, so none is drawn bold). `history_rows` (a pure function: the listed messages, oldest first, none twice) is covered by `cargo test`. While the workspace is rate-limited (the full read of S-5 found it so, `TimelineCache::rate_limited`) the command answers the error "Slack の呼び出し制限のため、しばらく読めません" at once and calls nothing; a 429 of its own call stops the workspace's reads for the wait Slack asked (`TimelineCache::limit`, as the full read does) and answers the same error (`history_failure`, covered by `cargo test`).

A message is drawn as a row of a group of the unread section is (S-8): the avatar, the name, the time, the text, its reactions (S-22) under the text when it has any, and "N 件の返信" under them when it has replies, with the bookmark of S-13 beside it, which is on when its thread is among the watched ones and watches or lets go of the thread as in S-13. A reply that was also sent to the channel (`subtype` `thread_broadcast`, with a `thread_ts` other than its own `ts`) has a line above its text, "スレッドに返信済み: {the start of the message it replied to}", muted (the color of the time) and cut with an ellipsis to one line (`.feed-thread-reply`). `slack_channel_history` gives it as `threadReply` (`ThreadReply`: `parent`), which is `null` for every other message and for every row of the unread list, which draws no such line. `parent` is the thread's own message as `headline` makes it (S-10's plain text, one line, at most 200 characters); the text is taken from the `root` that the message carries, else from the message of the same `conversations.history` answer whose `ts` is the `thread_ts`, and when neither is there (or its text is blank) `parent` is `null` and the line is "スレッドに返信済み" alone. Slack's documentation shows `root` only in the payload of the `thread_broadcast` message event (https://docs.slack.dev/reference/events/message/thread_broadcast); the pages of `conversations.history` and `conversations.replies` do not mention it, so whether the history carries it was not confirmed, and the message of the page is the fallback. `broadcast_of` (a pure function: the `root`, the page's parent, or neither) is covered by `cargo test`. The line takes no press: it lets the press through to the row.

Pressing the row or "N 件の返信" opens the thread of S-6 (`threadTs ?? ts`) in place of the history, and its back button shows the history again at the scroll position it had. The history opens at the newest message, at the bottom.

The history itself sets no read position. The press on the channel reads it (S-15), and that is the read of the history: a press on the same channel again reads the history once more and the channel again only if something is unread by then. Opening a thread from the history, or going back, reads nothing more. A thread opened from it is seen as S-6 and S-14 say of any thread. The history is read when the channel is pressed and after a post of S-21, not on a timer, and Socket Mode (S-16) does not change it; it is drawn again (not read again) when what it shows changes, such as the watched threads.

A history that cannot be read shows the error ("読み込めませんでした: …") in the body with "再読み込み", which reads it again; with messages already shown, a failed second read keeps them and shows the error above them. While the first read is on its way the body shows "読み込んでいます…".

The E2E spec `slack-list.e2e.ts` covers the call and its arguments, the header, the day separators, the messages, the line "スレッドに返信済み" of a reply also sent to the channel, their reactions and "N 件の返信", the channel pressed in the menu with the read of S-15 made at the press, the lock of a private channel, the opening at the newest message, opening a thread with the row and with "N 件の返信" and returning at the same position, the bookmark, the error with "再読み込み", and leaving for a section or when the channel is kept off the list.

## S-21 Post to a Slack channel

Under the history of a channel (S-20) is a field to post a message to that channel (`#sc-reply`, the same frame as the reply field of S-6): a multi-line text area (`#sc-input`, three lines high and growing to six, with no placeholder and no line of text that explains it; `aria-label` "チャンネルに投稿") and a button "投稿する" (`#sc-send`) at its right end below it. Enter is a line break, and Cmd+Enter (Ctrl+Enter off macOS) posts, unless it only confirms an IME conversion, as in S-6. The button is off while the text is blank (spaces and line breaks alone).

The post is `slack_channel_post` (`teamId`, `channel`, `text`, and an optional `markdown`), which posts the text as it is (`*` stays markup) when `markdown` is false or missing, with `chat.postMessage` under the user's own name, through the same Rust function as S-1 and S-6 (`send_post`: the scope `chat:write`, `unfurl_links` and `unfurl_media` off, the same Japanese errors), so that no new Slack method and no new scope is used. Rust checks that `teamId` is the ID of a connected workspace, that `channel` has the form of a channel ID and is one the workspace's channel list holds (the list the side menu is made of, as `slack_channel_history` checks it), and, in `post_text`, that the text is not blank and not past 10,000 characters (`POST_LIMIT`, the limit of S-1); nothing is sent otherwise. It is no thread reply, and the recent destinations of S-1 are not touched. The text goes to Slack only on the user's press or key.

When `markdown` is true (`slack_channel_post` and `slack_reply`, S-6), the text is Markdown and `slack_markdown.rs` (`to_mrkdwn`, a pure function) turns it into Slack's mrkdwn before the checks of `post_text`. It handles nine forms: bold (`*x*`), italic (`_x_`), strike (`~x~`), a link (`<url|text>`), a numbered list, a bulleted list, a quote (`> `), inline code and a code block (the language of a block is dropped); a heading becomes a bold line, and anything else (a table, HTML) stays as escaped text. `&`, `<` and `>` are escaped as `&amp;`, `&lt;` and `&gt;`, and nothing else is. Only an address with the scheme `http`, `https` or `mailto` becomes a link: any other, including one that starts with `!`, `@` or `#`, is dropped and the text alone stays, so that no Markdown link makes Slack notify `@channel` or a person. The limit of 10,000 characters is counted on the converted text, and so is the blank check. The screen does not pass `markdown` yet, so its posts are as before.

While the post is on its way, the field of that channel and the button are off, and the button of every channel stays off until it ends, so that a text is never posted twice. On success the field is empty and the history is read again (S-20's `slack_channel_history`) and scrolled to its newest message, where the message just posted shows; no read position is set. On a failure the error ("投稿できませんでした: …", from Rust) shows above the field and the text stays; typing or choosing another channel hides it. When the post ends after the history was left (another channel or a section chosen, or a thread shown), a toast tells the result instead ("#channel に投稿しました", "#channel に投稿できませんでした: …"), and a text that failed stays.

What is written is kept for each channel (`{teamId}/{channel}`) in memory only, until the app quits: choosing another channel and coming back finds it, a section or a thread in between does not lose it, and it is in no file or log.

The unit tests (`cargo test`) cover the check of the text of a post (`post_text`, S-1). The E2E spec `slack-list.e2e.ts` covers the call and its arguments with Cmd+Enter and with the button, Enter as a line break, the button off for a blank text, the field emptied and the history read again, the error with the text kept, the field and the button off while the post is on its way and a second press that posts nothing, and the text kept for each channel.

## S-22 Reactions in Slack messages

The thread (S-6) and the history of a channel (S-20) show the reactions of a message under it: for each reaction its emoji and how many people gave it (`🎉 3`), side by side in the order Slack gives them, wrapping to the next line when they do not fit. It is display only: no reaction is added or taken off, a reaction cannot be pressed, and who gave it is not shown. A message with no reaction draws nothing.

The reactions come from `reactions` of the message in `conversations.history` and `conversations.replies`, which cookapps already reads, so no new Slack method and no new scope is used. Rust (`reactions_of`) turns each entry into `Reaction` (`emoji`, `count`) on `ThreadMessage` and `TimelineMessage` as `reactions`, and the screen draws what it is given. The emoji is made from the entry's `name` as S-10 does (`emojify`): a standard emoji, with its skin tone (`+1::skin-tone-2`), is the Unicode emoji, and a custom emoji stays `:name:`. An entry with no `name` (or an empty one) or a `count` that is missing or not above 0 is left out, and so is a `reactions` that is not a list. The people who gave a reaction (`users`) are dropped from what Rust keeps of a message.

In the thread (`#st-msgs`) the reactions (`.reactions`, a `.reaction` for each with `.reaction-emoji` and `.reaction-count`) are the last part of the message, under its text, blocks, attachments, images and files. In the history (`#sc-feed`) they are under the text of the row and above "N 件の返信". They use the colors of `:root` and the font of the Slack screen (A-20).

The list (S-5), its section of watched threads (S-13) and the popups of S-17 do not draw them, although the rows carry the same `TimelineMessage`: those rows are a short summary of the messages to read next, and a row opens the thread, where the reactions are.

The unit tests (`cargo test`) cover `reactions_of` in `slack.rs`. The E2E specs `slack-thread.e2e.ts` and `slack-list.e2e.ts` cover the emoji and the count of each reaction in the order given, a custom emoji as `:name:`, the place under the message, nothing drawn for a message with none, the same in the history, and nothing drawn in the list.
