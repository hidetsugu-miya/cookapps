# Slack requirements

## S-1 Paste a mail into Slack

From the mail detail, the "Slack に貼る" button opens a screen of its own (not a popover) with the text to post in an edit field. The field starts as the subject, sender, date and body of the latest message (the body cut at 3,000 characters), and what is posted, under your own name, is the text in the field as the user edited it (`*` stays bold; blank or past 10,000 characters is refused). There is no separate note. The destination is chosen on the screen with a chip that opens the picker (the workspace, recent destinations, joined channels with a search, or a channel or thread link); none is chosen by itself, and "貼る" is enabled only with a destination and a non-blank text. "元の内容に戻す" (pressed twice) puts the starting text back. A post closes the screen, discards the draft and returns to the mail detail with a notice; a failure shows the error and keeps the text. "メールに戻る" returns to the mail detail (nothing is fetched again, the scroll position is restored), and the draft (text, destination and conversation of S-4) is kept in memory for one mail at a time, keyed by the thread and its latest message, so reopening the same mail finds it and another mail starts anew. You can have several workspaces. Authentication is a User OAuth Token per workspace (scopes `chat:write`, `channels:read`, `groups:read`); a Bot User OAuth Token (`xoxb-`) is refused when it is registered, and one already stored shows the same message in settings

## S-2 Summarize a Slack message into a Todo

Paste the URL of a Slack message into the Todo add field. cookapps fetches the whole thread of the message (`conversations.replies`, 200 messages a page, at most 20 pages, for a link to a reply as well as to the first message; a blank message is skipped except the linked one, a message a later page repeats is read once, a message's lines after the first are indented by two spaces so that nothing in a text can pass as a heading or a sender, and a later page that cannot be read ends the read there; a linked message past the page cap is read on its own, and the replies the cap or that failure left unread count as omitted, at least one unless only the linked reply was left, which was read on its own and counted by the thread's reply count), summarizes it with the Claude Code CLI (`claude -p`, the model and effort of A-2 with sonnet and medium as the default, no tools, no MCP, no settings), and shows the result on a card above the list. A thread whose lines, with their headings, fit 10,000 characters is sent whole in one call. A thread of one message that does not fit with its heading is that message alone, cut to 10,000 characters with its heading and the note below. A longer thread is split: the first message (2,000 characters at most; none when the thread's own message was blank) and the linked message (4,000 characters at most) and the latest replies (whole messages from the newest back, 3,000 characters; a single newer message over that is cut to it) go as they are, and the rest is grouped, from the newest back and at message boundaries, into chunks of at most 10,000 characters (at most 5, the ones nearest the end of the thread; a message over that is cut to it), each summarised by its own `claude -p` call (one at a time, the newest chunk first, with the same model and effort, a fixed Japanese instruction rather than the editable body of S-3, and the key points as the answer, as for M-8). The points reach the final call, at most 3,000 characters and never so many that the input passes 10,000, under "[途中の N 件の要点]" between the first message and the linked one (at the very start when the first message sent is one of the latest replies, so that it never comes between them; what a chunk leaves of its share of the characters goes to the chunks after it); the sections are headed "[スレッドの最初のメッセージ]", "[対象のメッセージ]" (what the task is about) and "[最新の返信]" (the present state), and "[スレッドの返信]" in a thread sent whole (its replies after the linked message show the present state too). When replies were left unread (the end of the thread was not read), the last message sent is followed by one fixed indented line ("（これより新しい返信は読めていません）"), so that the last message read is not taken for the end of the thread (if the line would pass 10,000 characters, that message is cut by the excess first). Every message that is cut, whichever part it is (first, linked, latest or a chunk's), is followed by one fixed indented line ("（このメッセージは長いため、ここで切っています。続きは読めていません）") in the text sent to Claude, and the whole stays within 10,000 characters. The chunk calls together have a time budget of 180 seconds, and only one chunk call runs at a time over all summaries (a slot of the two `claude -p` slots is left for M-8, S-3 and S-4): the wait for that one slot is part of the budget and a chunk that cannot get it before less than 30 seconds of the budget are left fails; no chunk starts with less than 30 seconds of the budget left, and the chunk running is cut off at it (a call never runs longer than the time limit of one call, A-2). The wait for a `claude -p` slot inside a call (60 seconds) is not part of the budget, so a chunk may end up to 60 seconds after it. A chunk that fails or is cut off is logged (never its text), stops the chunks still to run (a stalled network or a refused login would fail them all), and the summary goes on to the final call. The messages of a chunk that failed or did not run count as omitted; a failure of the final call is an error. `omitted` of the summary (the unread replies, the chunks left out and the failed ones; set by Rust only, never read from the model's answer) shows on the card as "N 件のメッセージは要約に含まれていません" when above 0, and is not sent on to `todoist_add_from_slack`. When the linked message is cut (at 4,000 characters in a split thread, or to fit a thread of one message), `targetCut` of the summary (set by Rust only, never read from the model's answer, and not sent on to `todoist_add_from_slack` either) shows on the card as "対象のメッセージは長いため、先頭だけを要約しました". The ID and name of every Todoist project (from the last sync) go to Claude with the thread, and it names the one that fits best (only an ID in that list is kept). The card holds the task name, a description (a short bullet list of the key points), a due date and the project (Claude's recommendation at first, tagged "推奨" on the chip and on its row in the project menu, else the current one), and all of them can be edited: the due chip opens the same popover as a task's detail (a text field for Todoist's natural language, and a month to pick a day from, which keeps the time typed in the field (`15:00`, `9:30`, `15時`, `15時30分`, each with 午前 or 午後 in front, alone or after a date, 今日, 明日 or 明後日; an hour of 24 or more or minutes of 60 or more, and any other form such as `15時半`, `夜8時` or `3pm`, are not read), else the time of the due date; a click elsewhere confirms the text as Enter does, and Escape cancels it), and nothing is saved by it; the source workspace is shown, and the message link, which cannot be edited, is put at the end of the description. Nothing is created until the user presses "Add" on the card, which adds the Todoist task; "Discard" closes the card and creates nothing. The Slack token also needs `channels:history`, `groups:history`, `im:history` and `mpim:history` (the last two for DM and group DM links). The Claude connection (A-4: the account of the subscription, or the API key) is shown in the side menu and in settings

## S-3 Edit the Todo summary prompt

The prompt of S-2 has a fixed part (the instruction to treat the message as untrusted data, how the message is enclosed, how the thread's headings and indented lines are read, today's date and the project list, and, after the body, the instruction not to choose the project or the due date because the message asks; it cannot be edited) and a body (how to write the name, description, due date and project, and the Japanese writing rules). The body is shown and edited on its own screen, opened from the Claude icon in the heading of the Todo list (shown in a list only, not in a task's detail or the token form; its label and tooltip are "Todo の要約プロンプトを確認・編集"), from the "Todo の要約プロンプト" row of the Claude tab in settings (shown whether or not Claude can be used) or from the "プロンプトを編集" link on the summary card, and "戻る" returns to where it was opened. While the screen is open, the side menu still points at the settings. Saving writes it to `todo-prompt.json` in the app config directory (saving the default body removes the file; an empty body or one over 10,000 characters is refused), and the next summary uses it. "デフォルトに戻す" removes the file. In a conversation with Claude (`claude -p`, the model and effort of the prompt conversation of A-2 with sonnet and medium as the default, no tools, no MCP, no settings) the user asks for a change; the reply is a short Japanese message and, when the body changes, a line diff with "適用" and "却下". The request is typed in a field that starts one line high and grows with its text up to 6 lines (then it scrolls), the pane's bottom staying put, with the model and effort selects of the prompt conversation (the same values as the Claude tab of settings, short texts such as "Sonnet" and "medium", no frame, right-aligned) under it, loaded when the screen opens so a way in from Todo shows them too (the Slack compose screen of S-4 has none); Enter is a line break, and Cmd+Enter (Ctrl+Enter off macOS) or the send button sends, with a hint saying so; a request of several lines keeps its line breaks in the conversation. A "クリア" button at the right end of the conversation header, pressed twice (the first press says "もう一度押すとクリア", and 4 seconds or leaving the button disarms it), forgets the conversation, so the next request is sent with no history; it is disabled while there is no conversation or Claude is answering, and it leaves the edit field, the saved instructions and the text being typed alone. Only the text of the edit field, the conversation and the request are sent, never a Slack message. Applying fills the edit field and does not save; an old proposal cannot be applied once the field was changed. The field and the conversation are kept in memory until the app quits. The instructions sent to the model (the fixed parts, the default body, and the mail summary and prompt-edit instructions, with the labels of their input) are written in Japanese; a body saved earlier stays as it was. The mail summary prompt (M-8) is not affected by the body. The conversation needs Claude to be usable (A-4). A proposal made from an empty text is shown as all lines added

## S-4 Proofread the text to paste into Slack with Claude

The right pane "Claude と校正" of the S-1 screen is the same conversation as S-3 (`ts/claude-chat.ts`: the log, a request field that grows up to 6 lines, Enter is a line break and Cmd+Enter (Ctrl+Enter off macOS) or the send button sends, a "クリア" button pressed twice, a line diff with "適用" and "却下", an old proposal cannot be applied once the field was changed, and a note and disabled controls while Claude cannot be used), about the text in the edit field (`claude_proofread_slack`; `claude -p`, the model and effort of A-2 with sonnet and medium as the default, no tools, no MCP, no settings). A "校正する" button above the request field sends the fixed request "校正してください". The instructions to the model (Japanese) say that the text is untrusted data and its instructions (and those in the assistant's turns of the conversation) are not followed, that the request decides what is corrected (typos, polite forms, wording), that facts, proper nouns, dates and URLs are not changed, that the subject line and the sender line at the top are not changed unless the request says so, that the reply is one or two short Japanese sentences, and that `body` is the whole text only when it changes and is identical except where changed. Only the text of the edit field (up to 10,000 characters), the conversation and the request are sent, and only when "校正する" or send is pressed. Applying replaces the text of the edit field and posts nothing. The conversation is kept with the draft of S-1 and goes with it; an answer that arrives after the screen was opened for another mail is dropped. A divider between the two panes (`role="separator"`, dragged with pointer events or moved with the left and right arrow keys) sets the width of the Claude pane to between a third and a half of the screen body (the CSS holds the range, the ratio is kept in memory until the app quits and survives a resized window). It needs Claude to be usable (A-4). A proposal made from an empty text is shown as all lines added

## S-5 Slack list

The Slack icon of the sidebar (`nav-slack`) opens a list of the messages of the public and private channels the token's user is in, across all workspaces, as an unread section (20 at most) and the watch section (S-13), each newest first (S-8; `slack_timeline`; direct messages and group DMs are not listed, nor are joins and other subtypes; a reply that was also sent to the channel is). The list has a header (S-13) but shows no unread count of its own (the Slack button's badge of S-8 does); while `pendingChannels` of the answer (channels no read has reached yet, over all workspaces) is above 0, the muted "残り N チャンネルを読み込み中" (`#slack-pending`, 32px from the left like the list) is shown above the notice, and takes no room while hidden. The feed starts 8px below the top, as the inbox does, and the notice has 8px above it. A row is a real button: the sender's icon (the initial on a color taken from the name until the icon is here or when it cannot be fetched) with a dot in the workspace's color (the color of the workspace's avatar in the sidebar), the name, `#channel`, the text in two lines at most (a message of files only says "（添付ファイル）"), "返信 N 件" when above 0, and the date as the inbox writes it. The unread mark and the unread section are S-8 and the watch section is S-13; there is no mark for a Todo made, no attached image, and no hover action but the two buttons of S-9 and S-13. One read asks `conversations.history` for at most 40 channels of a workspace (not the ones kept off the list, S-9): the channels never read first, then the ones read longest ago; the others show the messages their last read kept in memory (`TimelineCache`), so a workspace of more channels is covered over several reads and the answer takes a bounded time. A rate limit (429) is not retried in this read: it ends that workspace's read for the round and stops its reads until Slack's `Retry-After` has passed (60 seconds when it gives none, 10 minutes at most), while the others are read and the kept messages are still listed. A channel never read that fails stays pending and is tried in the next round. The list is read at startup (`slack_load` first; with no workspace nothing is called) and again every `refreshSecs` of the answer (120 seconds; 60 seconds while a channel is unread, a workspace has over 40 channels or a watched thread is not checked yet, S-13) while the window is visible (nothing is read while it is hidden, and it is read at once on the return when the list held is that old), on whichever screen is shown, by the one read of `ts/slack-list.ts`, so that whether the Slack screen is shown does not double it and only the latest read is used (the badge of S-8 follows it). Showing the Slack screen reads only when no read is on its way and the list held was read `refreshSecs` of its answer ago or longer (otherwise it is drawn as it is; a workspace added or removed in between, in settings or by `slack_load`, makes it stale at once, and when the last one is gone the rows, the failure and the badge are dropped; a failed `slack_load` keeps the workspaces known, so the tray and the refresh stay). A read while another screen is shown draws nothing of the list (it is drawn from what is held when the screen is shown), never calls `show()` and never switches the screen. An answer replaces the rows in place (the scroll position and the focused row stay, `show()` is not called). A workspace that could not be read, and the scopes a token lacks (`users:read`, `files:read`, `channels:write` and `groups:write` are optional), show as a notice above the list with "設定を開く" (the Slack tab) while the rows that were read stay (a workspace that could not be read still lists the messages its earlier reads kept, and a read that fails altogether keeps the rows and shows its error in the notice). The first read shows a spinner; with no workspace the list says so with "設定を開く"; a failed read with nothing to show (the call failed, or every connected workspace failed and there is no message) says so with "再読み込み"; when only some workspaces failed, the notice above the list says which and the empty list says "メッセージはありません" (also when the call failed while a list with no message is held). Messages, names and icons are in memory only. Sender icons and the user's own icons are fetched by `fetch_images` (no token) as data: URIs, so the CSP stays; Rust gives an icon URL only when it is https and its host is `slack-edge.com` or a subdomain of it or `secure.gravatar.com` (a bot's, a user's; any other host gives no icon and the initial shows). A read asks `users.info` about at most 40 users, chosen after the ones the cache answers (an hour; five minutes for one Slack could not describe) are left out, and once a call finds `users:read` missing, or the scopes remembered for the workspace say so, the rest of that call's lookups are skipped; each workspace's own user ID (`auth.test`) is asked once and remembered in memory until the workspace is removed. The sender names and icons need `users:read`, the attached images `files:read` (settings shows what a token lacks of them without calling the workspace short of permission, and the setup hint lists the scopes)

## S-6 Slack thread

A row opens the thread in place of the list, filling the content area, with a back button that returns to the list at the scroll position it had and reads nothing more than the normal refresh (the list keeps being read in the background and the thread stays). The tinted header has the back button, `#channel` with the workspace name muted, "Slack で開く" (a link to the permalink, which the default browser opens through Rust's navigation hook) and "Todo にする". The messages (`slack_thread` with the workspace, the channel and the thread's own timestamp, `threadTs ?? ts`) are drawn oldest first: a 40px avatar, the name, the time and the text as text (only URLs are linked, by `linkify`, and the styles of S-12 are drawn; a labeled Slack link shows as `label (URL)` when the label is not the URL), and the blocks and attachments of S-11 where the message has them, and after the first message the separator "返信 N 件" when there are replies. Attached images show as thumbnails under the text: a placeholder until `slack_file_images` answers (the token goes to `files.slack.com` only, in Rust; the images are kept in memory per workspace in a cache of their own in `SlackState`, which `fetch_images` cannot read and removing the workspace clears), then the image linked to the file in Slack; a file that is not an image, and an image that could not be fetched, show as a chip with the file name that opens the file in Slack. A thread that cannot be read shows the error in the pane with "再読み込み". "Todo にする" (shown only while Claude can be used and Todoist has a token) summarises the message by its permalink (`todoist_summarize_slack`, the same as S-2) and shows the card of S-2 above the reply field, without the link to the prompt screen; "追加" calls `todoist_add_from_slack` and shows "Todo に追加しました", "破棄" closes the card. The reply field (placeholder "スレッドに返信", three lines high, growing to six; Enter is a line break, Cmd+Enter (Ctrl+Enter off macOS) or "返信する" posts, which is off while the field is blank) posts with `slack_reply` (the checks and the errors of S-1; the recent destinations of S-1 are not touched). On success the field empties, the thread is read again and scrolled to the end, and "スレッドに返信しました" shows; on a failure the error shows on the thread and the text stays; the field and the button wait while the post is on its way. When the read stopped before the end of the thread (`truncated`), "これより新しい返信は読めていません" ends the messages. Opening the same thread again keeps its Todo card, and a reply or a summary that ends after the thread was left and opened again is applied to it. The reply and the conversation of S-7 are kept in memory per thread (the workspace, the channel and the thread's timestamp) until the app quits

## S-7 Ask Claude about the thread

The right pane "Claude" of the S-6 screen is a general conversation about the thread and the reply, on the conversation of S-4 (`ts/claude-chat.ts`: the log, the growing request field, Cmd+Enter (Ctrl+Enter off macOS) or the send button, "クリア" pressed twice, a line diff with "適用" and "却下", an old proposal cannot be applied once the field changed, and a note and disabled controls while Claude cannot be used). Its header is the Claude icon and "クリア" only (no title), the request field says "Claude に頼みたいことを書く", the send button is an up-arrow icon (`aria-label` "送信", an `.icon-btn` made round in the accent color; the send buttons of S-3 and S-4 stay text buttons), there is no "校正する" button, and neither the pane nor the reply field tells the keys (the keys still work). An open proposal has "却下", "コピー" and "適用" (in that order; "コピー" copies the whole proposed text with the clipboard of the Todo menus and the notice "コピーしました"). A request is `claude_chat_thread` (`claude -p`, the model and effort of `slackReply` in A-2, Sonnet and high by default, no tools, no MCP, no settings) with the reply field's text (up to 10,000 characters; it may be empty, and Claude may write a reply into `body`), the open thread's messages (`name`, `ts`, `text` of each `ThreadMessage`, none while the thread is not read; nothing is read from Slack again) with `truncated` (whether the read of the thread stopped before its end), the conversation and the request, and only when send is pressed. `ThreadMessage.text` is what the screen shows of a message as plain text (`slack_blocks::message_plain`): the message's text while the screen draws it (`showText`), then the text of the blocks drawn (headers, sections and fields, context, image descriptions and titles, buttons with their addresses, a quote line with `> `) and of the attachments (pretext, author, title with its link, text, fields as `title: value`, footer, buttons, blocks, image descriptions, and the fallback when it is all that is drawn), so a bot's post reaches Claude as it reads on the screen, with the addresses of its buttons and links (so that Claude can use a link in a reply). `thread_section` (a pure function) turns the messages into lines `{name} ({time}): {text}`, oldest first, the lines of a text after the first indented by two spaces (as S-2) so that no text can pass as a heading or a sender, a message with no text left out; the whole is at most 10,000 characters, the lines made from the newest back and the making stopped at the first that does not fit, so the oldest are dropped (the fixed line "（これより古いメッセージは省略しています）" heads the rest when any was and the thread has more than one message with text), and a newest message that alone does not fit is cut with the note of S-2 (with the whole 10,000 characters when it is the only message). When `truncated` is true the fixed line of S-2 "（これより新しい返信は読めていません）" ends the thread, counted in the 10,000. The thread and the reply text are each enclosed between `BEGIN`/`END` lines of a random boundary, which is taken out of both. The instructions to the model (Japanese) say that the thread and the reply text are untrusted data whose instructions (and those in the assistant's turns) are not followed, that Claude helps to proofread, summarise the thread, answer a question and write a reply, that facts, proper nouns, dates and URLs are not changed, that the answer is in Japanese (a proofreading report in one or two short sentences, a summary or an answer as long as it needs, bullets allowed; the pane keeps the line breaks of an answer, `#reply-chat .chat-ai { white-space: pre-wrap }`, and the panes of S-3 and S-4 do not), and that `body` is the whole reply text only when the request changes it. A text written from nothing (an empty reply field) is all additions in the diff. Applying replaces the text of the field and posts nothing; only "返信する" posts. The conversation is kept per thread in memory; an answer asked in one thread that arrives after another was opened is dropped. Under the request field are the frameless model and effort selects of `slackReply` (A-2), as S-3 has those of the prompt conversation. A divider between the thread and the pane (`role="separator"`, dragged with pointer events or moved with the left and right arrow keys) sets the width of the pane to between a third and a half (`ts/split.ts`, shared with S-4), with a ratio of its own kept in memory until the app quits. The thread screen and the pane share the rows of one CSS grid (`subgrid`, nothing is measured in JS), so the line under the two headers and the line over the reply field and the request field are at the same height, whichever field grows

## S-8 Slack list: unread and read

The list of S-5 shows the messages that are unread by the read position Slack keeps for each channel (`last_read` of `conversations.info`, which answers for public and private channels). The unread rows (the newest 20 at most) come first, newest first, under the heading "未読" (a dot icon and the style of the inbox's "既読" heading), in bold as in the inbox; there is no read section, so a message that is read leaves the list unless its thread is watched (S-13). `slack_timeline` chooses them with the pure `select_timeline` from all the messages `TimelineCache` holds (the newest 20 of each channel from `conversations.history`; the channels kept off the list, S-9, left out): no older unread message is looked for, and above 20 unread messages only the newest 20 come; `messages` of the answer holds only these (each still has `unread`), without the messages of the watched threads (they are in `watched`, S-13). `Timeline.markableChannels` is the number of channels S-13's "すべて既読にする" would move. There is no heading with the count: the number of unread messages is the Slack button's badge only. The screen shows the first 10 unread rows; when more follow, a centered `.text-btn` "未読をあと N 件表示" (N the rest) comes right after them, and pressing it shows all of them (20 at most) and removes it. It stays open through a background read and the return from a thread, and goes back to 10 when Slack is opened again from another screen. A section with no row is not drawn, nor is its heading ("未読", "ウォッチ"). The scroll position and the focus stay on a background read, which never calls `show()`. A message is unread when its `ts` is later than the channel's `last_read`, the two compared as numbers and not as text (`is_unread`, `ts_key`), unless the sender is the user (the workspace's own user ID, `auth.test`, asked once and remembered, as for the icons); a channel whose `last_read` is not known has no unread message, and a reply inside a thread is not in the list and is not counted (`TimelineMessage.unread`). After `newest_first` and before choosing, `slack_timeline` asks `conversations.info` for the channels the cache holds messages of, at most `INFO_BUDGET` (40) per workspace that could be read in one read (`choose_info_channels`: first the channels whose `last_read` is not known or whose newest message held is later than it, then the rest with what is left of the budget; within each, the channels never asked, then the ones asked longest ago, by a time kept per channel apart from the value, so that a channel that gives no `last_read` cannot keep the front; those past the budget use the value kept), `HISTORY_CONCURRENCY` calls at a time for each workspace, and keeps each `last_read` and when it was set in `TimelineCache` in memory only, until the workspace is removed; a failed call keeps the earlier value (a value Slack says replaces the kept one, also an earlier one when the user marked the channel unread there, but the answer of a call that began before the value was set, as by opening a message, is dropped: `put_last_read_started`). The workspace's own user ID (`auth.test`) is asked without a retry on this path. When a row is opened while a read is on its way, the list is made again from the cache (as in S-9, no call to Slack) once `slack_mark_read` is done, so the old answer does not leave the rows unread. The names and icons are asked about only for the rows listed (the unread rows, 20 at most, and the watched ones). A 429 is not retried: it ends that workspace's `conversations.info` calls for the round and stops them until `Retry-After` has passed (`info_limited_until`, the method's own limit, apart from the `conversations.history` stop of S-5, so the history reads go on). Pressing a row of the list sets the channel's read position to that message: the screen takes the row, and the rows of the same team and channel not newer than it (compared as numbers), out of the unread section at once (a watched row stays in the watch section and is no longer bold), and calls `slack_mark_read` (`teamId`, `channel`, `ts`), which sends `conversations.mark` and then moves the kept `last_read` forward, only when `ts` is later than the kept `last_read` (`moves_forward`: the position never moves back, and a read row, or a channel with no known position, sends nothing). A failure is logged by Rust and ignored by the screen, which shows Slack's value at the next read. The scopes `channels:write` and `groups:write` are optional (`OPTIONAL_SCOPES`, in `TIMELINE_SCOPES`, so `missing` of the answer lists them and the notice of S-5, the status of the workspace in settings and the scope list of the `?` hint name them): when the token of the message's workspace lacks either, the press calls nothing and changes nothing on the screen. `users.prefs.get` and muted channels are not read. The Slack button (`nav-slack`) has a `.nav-count` badge like the inbox's and Todo's: not shown for 0, `aria-label` "Slack（未読 N 件）", and "20+" above 20 (`setNavCount` with the cap 20). The number is `unreadTotal` of the answer, which Rust counts over all the messages held (not only the ones listed; the channels kept off the list, the user's own messages and the channels with no known `last_read` not counted, by the same `is_unread`), and it updates on every read of S-5, on any screen. A read that fails keeps the number. The screen lowers it at once when a row is opened (by the rows that move to read) and when a channel is hidden (by its unread rows listed); the next read gives the exact number. The badge is not added to the Dock badge (`set_badge`)

## S-9 Keep a channel off the Slack list

A row of the list shows, while it is hovered or has the focus, an `.icon-btn` (an eye with a slash) in the place of the date, which is `visibility: hidden` then; its `aria-label` is "#channel を一覧に出さない". It is a sibling of the row (a `.feed-item` holds both), so a button is not put in a button. Pressing it opens no thread and sets no read position. The rows of that channel go from the unread section at once (a watched thread of the channel stays in the watch section, S-13), `slack_hide_channel` (`teamId`, `channel`) saves it, the notice "#channel を一覧に出さないようにしました" with "元に戻す" is shown (the notice part of the inbox's undo), and the list is made again from Rust's cache; "元に戻す" calls `slack_unhide_channel` and makes it again the same way; a failed save shows the error and makes it again the same way, which brings the rows back. That is `slack_timeline` with `cached: true`: it calls nothing of Slack (no `conversations.history`, `conversations.info`, `users.info` or `auth.test`, no token read), so hiding or putting back many channels one after another cannot reach Slack's rate limits. It makes the list again, with the same functions as a full read (`held_messages`, `pick_timeline`, `timeline_messages`), from the messages `TimelineCache` holds (a channel put back shows its messages only if the cache still holds them; its history was dropped by the read after it was hidden, so otherwise it comes with the next full read), the read positions and names kept, and the hidden and watched files; `failed` and `missing` are those of the last full read (for the workspaces still connected, `put_notice`), and `pendingChannels` and `refreshSecs` are computed from the channel lists kept in memory (the channels kept off the list left out; with no list kept, 0). The periodic read and the first read are full reads as before, and a full read loads the hidden file again after its calls so that a channel hidden meanwhile is not listed or counted. On the screen the latest request is the only one used: saving the file passes over every request made before it, no answer is used while a save is on its way, and when a full read that was passed over ends (its calls left what it read in the cache) one cached read follows, so a read that began before a channel was hidden cannot bring its rows back and its data is not lost. Putting a channel back from the settings card makes the list again from the cache once too (not a full read). The channels are kept in `slack-hidden.json` in the app config directory (`{"hidden":[{"teamId","channel"}]}`, IDs only, written through a temporary file and a rename as the other lists are; a file that cannot be read, or an ID that is not valid, counts as not there). A hidden channel is not asked of `conversations.history` or `conversations.info` (no budget is used), is not counted in `pendingChannels` or in the channel count of the refresh interval, has no message in the answer or in `unreadTotal` (also one a read kept before it was hidden: `visible_channels`, `visible_messages`), and its history is dropped from the cache. Removing the workspace removes its entries. Slack's own mute is not read (the API gives no access to it) and is not followed. The Slack tab of settings has a card "一覧に出さないチャンネル" after the connection card (the Slack tab panel is a plain panel with two cards): a count next to the heading ("N 件"), and per channel a dot in the workspace's color, `#channel` (the channel ID alone when its name is not known: `slack_hidden_list` takes the name from the channel list the app holds in memory, and Slack is not asked), the workspace's name (muted) and a "戻す" button (`aria-label` "#channel を一覧に戻す") which calls `slack_unhide_channel`, reads the card again and makes the Slack list again from the cache (`cached: true`, within a second); with none, "一覧に出さないチャンネルはありません"

## S-10 Emoji in Slack text

The text of the Slack list (S-5) and of the thread (S-6) shows an emoji code (`:tada:`) as the Unicode emoji (🎉). Rust does it in `plain_text` (`emojify`: the `emojis` crate and its shortcodes first, then `slack_emoji.rs`, a generated table of the Slack names the crate lacks, taken from iamcal/emoji-data (MIT), so that `:thinking_face:` and `:flag-jp:` work too), for the text of a message only, so the screen draws what it is given. `:+1::skin-tone-2:` becomes the emoji with that tone (2 to 6); an emoji with no tone gets the base emoji, and the code is not left. A code that is no emoji of ours (a custom emoji) stays as `:name:`. The inside of a `<...>` (a link, a mention, a channel), of an inline code (closed on its own line) and a code block (found in the whole message, so a code may hold a `<...>`), and of a web address is not converted, and neither is a code right after an ASCII letter or digit, such as the time `10:30:45` or `host:id:123`. The text sent to Claude for the Todo summary (S-2) and the text of the mail paste (S-1) are not converted

## S-11 Blocks and attachments in the thread

The thread of S-6 draws the `blocks` (Block Kit) and `attachments` of a message, as bots and apps put their content there and keep only a short sentence in `text`. The list (S-5) and what is sent to Claude (S-2, S-7) stay on `text`. `slack_blocks.rs` turns Slack's JSON into the structure the screen draws (`ThreadMessage.blocks`, `attachments` and `imageUrls`, ts-rs types; `ts/slack-blocks.ts` only builds the nodes, and the screen decides nothing). A message with a block other than `rich_text` that can be drawn shows its blocks and not its `text` (the alternative text), as Slack does; the `rich_text` blocks of that message are flattened to paragraphs. `text` is drawn too, under the blocks (`ThreadMessage.showText`), when the message has no block to draw and when a block of it could not be drawn (an unknown type, a select menu only, an empty section; the text may stand for it). A person's ordinary post, which is `rich_text` only (it repeats `text`), shows `text` as before and its `rich_text` is not even flattened; a bot's post (`bot_profile` or `username`) or one with no text draws its `rich_text` as paragraphs. `message_blocks` looks at the blocks other than `rich_text` first, and flattens `rich_text` only when it will be drawn. Blocks drawn: header, section (text, fields, and a button or an image as accessory), context (texts and small images), divider, image, actions (buttons) and markdown (its text as it is, as a section); any other type, a select menu and a block with nothing in it are left out. An attachment is drawn under the text or the blocks: a color bar (`color` as a hex or `good`, `warning` or `danger`; any other value gives none), pretext, author_name, title (a link only for an http or https `title_link`), text, fields (title and value), its own `image_url` and `thumb_url` (both when both are given, under the image rules below, with the `fallback` as their description), the blocks inside it, the buttons of its actions, and footer; `fallback` is drawn as a text only when nothing else of the attachment is, and an image counts as something drawn (an attachment of an image alone is kept, and its `fallback` is only the image's description). Texts go through `plain_text` (mrkdwn, S-10's emoji, `label (URL)`; a `plain_text` object is not mrkdwn: it gets `&amp; &lt; &gt;` undone and emoji only when it says `emoji`), are put into the page as text, and only URLs are links (`linkify`: the header, the title without a link, the author, the footer and a field's title too; a title that is a link holds none). The names in `<@U…>` and in `user` elements are looked up with the sender's (`mentioned_users`, read only in the drawn parts: text, fields, elements, blocks, pretext, title, footer, author_name and a field's value, and an attachment's `fallback` only when it is what is drawn). A button with an http or https `url` is an `a.text-btn` that the navigation hook opens in the default browser (`navigation_of` already hands http and https to it); any other scheme, and the app's own origin (`tauri.localhost`, which the hook lets through to the window itself), gives no `url`. A link button and a linked title have their address as `title` (the tooltip). A button with no `url` shows its name and cannot be pressed. Images are not fetched by default. Rust gives an image's `url` only when it is https and not on `files.slack.com` (which needs the token; a `slack_file` image has none), and lists them in `imageUrls`. A message with any shows a "画像を表示" button; pressing it calls `fetch_images` (no token, data: URIs, public https hosts, 50 URLs a call, as the mail does) for that message's images, and makes again the row of that message only (`replaceWith`; the other rows and the scroll position stay). An image with no `url`, or one `fetch_images` could not fetch, is drawn as its `alt` text; one waiting for the press is not drawn. The pressed messages and the fetched images are kept in memory while the same thread is open, read again (after a reply) or opened again, and forgotten when another thread is opened. A failed call keeps the button and shows a toast. The CSP is not changed. Select menus, input and other interactive parts are not drawn. The styles of a mrkdwn text are S-12

## S-12 Slack styles in the thread

The thread of S-6 draws the styles of Slack's mrkdwn in the text of a message: `*bold*` (`<strong>`), `_italic_` (`<em>`), `~strike~` (`<s>`), `` `code` `` (`<code>`), a block between ``` marks (`<pre><code>`, scrolling sideways on a long line) and lines that start with `&gt;` (`<blockquote>`). The list (S-5, `TimelineMessage`) and what is sent to Claude (S-2, S-7) stay on plain text. The screen of the thread draws `ThreadMessage.rich`; `ThreadMessage.text` stays as a string that is not read for styles. `slack_mrkdwn.rs` reads the raw text of Slack (not the output of `plain_text`, in which the range of a `<…>` and a `&gt;` are lost) into `ThreadMessage.rich` (`RichBlock`: `paragraph` and `quote` hold `Span`s with `bold`, `italic`, `strike` and `code`, `code` holds a text; ts-rs types), and `ts/slack-rich.ts` only builds the nodes (`textContent` and `linkify`, never `innerHTML`; the elements are made with the DOM API, so an HTML tag in a text stays a text). Order: the code blocks are cut out first (the line break that sets a block off from the text beside it belongs to neither; a ``` with no partner is text; a block of blanks is dropped); the rest goes by lines, a run of lines that start with `&gt;` (and one space) is one quote and the other lines one paragraph, with the line breaks kept in the text and drawn by `white-space: pre-wrap`; in a line, an inline code (closed on the line, not empty), a `<…>` token and a web address are indivisible, and the marks are read around them. A mark works as Slack's does, at the edge of a word and on one line: it opens when it is at the start of the line or after white space or any character but an ASCII letter or digit and the next character is not white space, and it closes at the nearest mark of its kind that follows a non-space and is followed by the end, white space or any character but an ASCII letter or digit, so `snake_case` and `2*3*4` are not styled, a mark with no partner is text, and styles nest (`*_x_*`). A Japanese letter beside a mark does not stop it (not checked against Slack, which the owner cannot compare with; the sample messages are Japanese). A mark at the end of a web address (and the punctuation after it) is not part of the address (`*https://a.example*` is a bold link), and a mark inside one is text. The text of every piece goes through the same functions as `plain_text` (`unescape`, `emojify` of S-10, `token_text`), so the mentions, links and emoji are as before. Inside a code (inline or block) no mark and no emoji is read; `&lt; &gt; &amp;` are undone and a `<…>` is read by `token_text` as in `plain_text`. A `linkify` link is made in every style but not in a code. Which texts are read: the `text` of a message (not when it was posted with `mrkdwn: false`), the `mrkdwn` objects of a section (text and fields) and of a context, and, in an attachment, `pretext`, `text` and the `value` of the fields only when `mrkdwn_in` names `text`, `pretext` or `fields` (Slack's rule). The texts of type `plain_text`, the header, the buttons, the title, footer and author of an attachment, the `rich_text` blocks (flattened, with their quote mark as text) and the `markdown` block are not read, and their marks stay. `>>>` (a quote to the end of the message; a line that starts with it is text) and the contents of an attached `.md` file are not read. The blank and spacing of the text are as before. The colors are the variables of `:root` (`--code`, `--code-line`, `--chip-line`)

## S-13 Watch threads and the header of the Slack list

Under a header fixed above the list (the tint of the thread header `#st-head`; `#slack-header`), the list has two sections, "未読" (S-8) above "ウォッチ", with no tab. The header has the title "Slack" at the left and, at the right, "すべて既読にする" and an `.icon-btn` (`aria-label` "一覧に出さないチャンネル") that opens the Slack tab of settings. A section with no row is not drawn. **Watch**: the unit is the thread (`threadTs ?? ts`) and the row shows the thread's own message. A row shows, beside the S-9 button and while it is hovered or has the focus, a bookmark button (`.feed-watch`, `aria-label` "ウォッチする") that watches its thread (`slack_watch`: it reads the thread once with `conversations.replies` and starts the count of new replies at its newest reply, so the replies that exist are not new; with `seenTs` nothing is asked of Slack and the thread is put back as it was). The messages of a watched thread (the thread's own message, and a reply also sent to the channel) are listed in the watch section only, never in the unread section, and the thread's unread messages still count in S-8's `unreadTotal` (that number is not changed). The screen moves the row at once and reads the list again from Rust's cache (`cached: true`); on a failed save it shows the error and brings the row back the same way, and the requests go through the same guard as S-9 (the latest request only, none answered while a save is on its way). A watched row has its bookmark always shown (filled, `aria-label` "ウォッチを外す") and the S-9 button on hover; pressing the bookmark lets the thread go (`slack_unwatch`, which answers the `seen_ts` it had) and the notice "ウォッチを外しました" with "元に戻す" puts it back with `slack_watch` and that `seenTs`. A watched row is bold when its message is unread (the position of its channel, as S-8, and the `last_read` of a channel the cache holds no message of is not known, so it is not unread). The section is ordered by the newest activity (the thread's own `ts` or its latest reply) first. A watched thread stays until it is let go, also when its channel is kept off the list (S-9); removing the workspace removes its threads. **New replies**: a row shows "新しい返信 N 件" (N above 0) for the replies of others (the user's own, by the workspace's own user ID, are not counted) after `seenTs`; a thread longer than the first page (`truncated`: a cursor or `has_more`) whose newest reply (`latest_reply`) is after `seenTs` has new replies that cannot be counted (`WatchMark.uncounted`; `newReplies` is 0): the row says "新しい返信あり" without a number (a latest reply of the user's own is not told apart there). Opening a watched row clears the count at once and calls `slack_watch_seen`, and the thread detail (S-6) calls it with `latestTs`, the newest reply `slack_thread` answered (when the thread has replies, at each read of it, the reload included): it moves `seenTs` forward (`with_seen`, never back) to the later of that and the newest reply the last check found (`seen_target`), does nothing for a thread that is not watched, and asks Slack nothing. Opening a watched row also sets the channel's read position (`slack_mark_read`, with `moves_forward` and the write scopes as S-8 says) to `WatchMark.unreadTo`, the newest unread message the cache holds of the thread (its own message, or a reply also sent to the channel; `thread_unread_to`), so a reply sent to the channel is read with the thread; the badge (`unreadTotal`) is defined as before. **Data**: `slack-watched.json` in the app config directory holds `{"watched":[{"teamId","channel","ts","seenTs"}]}` (IDs and timestamps only, written through a temporary file and a rename as the other lists are; a file that cannot be read, an ID or a timestamp that is not valid counts as not there, and a `seenTs` that is not valid becomes the thread's `ts`); the text, names and icons are in memory only (`TimelineCache.watch`) and are read again after a start. There is no upper limit on the watched threads; each full read of `slack_timeline` checks at most `WATCH_BUDGET` (20) of them over all workspaces with `conversations.replies` (the first page of 200: the thread's own message, then the replies), the ones never checked first and then the ones checked longest ago (`choose_watch_checks`), `HISTORY_CONCURRENCY` calls at a time for each workspace, in the same read (no screen or timer of its own). A 429 is not retried: it ends that workspace's checks for the round and stops them until `Retry-After` has passed (`replies_limited_until`). The checks run beside the read of the read positions (`tokio::join!`), after the user's own ID of each workspace is known. `slack_watch` is stopped by the same wait (it answers an error at once while it lasts, calls `conversations.replies` without a retry, and starts the wait on a 429). A check that fails keeps what the last good one found. A thread Slack says cannot be read (`thread_not_found`, `channel_not_found`, `not_in_channel`, `message_not_found`, or an answer without the thread's own message) is kept as a row that says so (`watch.status` "unreadable": the text "このスレッドは読めません…", the row not pressable) with its bookmark, so it can be let go; a thread not checked yet (or whose checks failed so far) is "pending" ("読み込んでいます…"), and while any is pending `refreshSecs` is 60 (`REFRESH_BUSY_SECS`, `unchecked_watch_rows`), as it is while channels are unread, and 120 otherwise. Only the workspaces that could be read in the round are checked. `slack_watch` takes the file lock first and then reads the workspace list again, and saves nothing (and answers `NO_WORKSPACE`) for a workspace that has been removed. `slack_watch_seen` returns at once, without the lock, for a thread the cache has no check of; the screen reads the list again from the cache (`cached: true`) after it when a read was on its way, as `slack_mark_read` does. `WatchMark {newReplies, uncounted, status, unreadTo}` is the `watch` of a `TimelineMessage` of the section (null in the unread section). **すべて既読にする (Slack)**: the pure `mark_targets` takes all the messages the cache holds (not only the ones listed; the channels kept off the list are not in them) and gives, for each channel with an unread message of a workspace whose token may write the position (`lacks_mark_scope`: the scopes last seen lack neither `channels:write` nor `groups:write`; unknown scopes are not a lack), the newest unread message's `ts`. The button is disabled while `markableChannels` is 0. It takes two presses (the second click of a double click, `detail` above 1, is ignored, so a double click only arms it): the first says "もう一度押すとすべて既読" (4 seconds or leaving the button disarms it, as "クリア" of S-3 does); the second makes the rows go at once (the unread rows of the workspaces that may write, the watched rows no longer bold, the badge lowered, `markableChannels` 0) and calls `slack_mark_all_read`, which sends `conversations.mark` for each target (`HISTORY_CONCURRENCY` at a time for each workspace; a 429 is not retried and ends that workspace's calls for the round, the channels not reached counting as failed; the 429 starts a wait of its own, `mark_limited_until`, until `Retry-After` has passed, in which that workspace is not sent to and its channels count as failed) and moves the held `last_read` only for a channel Slack accepted (`advance_last_read`, so the position never moves back, as `moves_forward`). It answers `{marked, failed}`; the list is then made again from the cache (`cached: true`), where a channel that could not be moved is unread still, and `failed` above 0 shows an error.
