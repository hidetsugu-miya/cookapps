# App requirements

## A-3 Settings tabs

The settings screen has eight tabs, in order Google, カレンダー, Todoist, Slack, Claude, Orca, フォント and アップデート (`role="tablist"`), and shows the cards of the selected tab only (the Slack tab has two, the connection and the channels kept off the list of S-9; the Claude tab has three: the connection of A-4, the model and effort of A-2, and the prompt row of S-3, in a tab panel that is no card itself, so the panel keeps its `role="tabpanel"`; the Orca tab has three, in a tab panel that is no card itself, the terminal colors of A-14, the editor of A-18 and the Ghostty settings of A-13; the フォント tab has one, the fonts of A-20). The side menu's settings button opens it on Google, and so does a way in that names no tab; the "設定を開く" of the Claude popup of the side menu opens it on Claude, and the ways to add a Slack workspace (the notice of the Slack compose screen, and the failed Slack summary of the Todo add field) open it on Slack. "戻る" of the prompt screen returns to the tab it was opened from. Left and right arrow keys move between the tabs (the first and the last are neighbours). Selecting a tab closes an open `?` hint and scrolls the settings to the top. The heading "設定" and the tabs stay at the top (`position: sticky`) while a card scrolls under them; only the underline of the tabs divides them, with no shadow. A red dot is on the アップデート tab while a newer version is out (U-1)

## A-5 Side menu by screen

The sidebar runs, top to bottom: inbox, calendar, the Google accounts (`#account-list`), Todo, Slack (`nav-slack`), the Slack workspaces (`#slack-workspace-list`), Orca (`nav-orca`, the terminal icon, with the count of A-6), then the bottom group (Claude, settings). Each list sits in a tray (background `--side-hover`, radius 12px, 28px round avatars; an empty tray is hidden) under the icon of its function and shows only while that function is on screen: the Google accounts while the screen is the inbox (list, thread, the Slack compose screen of S-1) or the calendar, the Slack workspaces while the screen is the Slack list or thread; Todo, Orca and settings show neither, and the prompt screen follows the screen it was reached from. The popup of an avatar closes when the screen changes. A Google avatar opens the account popup beside itself as before; a workspace avatar opens the same popup with the workspace name, the user, a status label (what the last read of the list found: 接続済み, 読み込み失敗, 権限が不足) and "設定を開く" (the Slack tab), its tooltip is the workspace name, and its picture is the user's own icon there (`slack_icons`, fetched through `fetch_images`), the initial on the workspace's color until it is here. The column of channels at the left of the Slack screen (the side menu of S-19) is a part of that screen and not of this sidebar

## A-20 Fonts by screen

The settings screen has a tab 「フォント」 (between Orca and アップデート, A-3) in which the family and the size of the text of each screen, of the whole app and of the monospace text (the family only) are chosen. A choice is drawn at once and saved in the app config directory. Rust is `src-tauri/src/fonts.rs` (the commands `font_settings`, `font_families` and `font_settings_set`); the frontend is `ts/font-settings.ts` (the card, and the application of the choice), `ts/orca-terminal.ts` (the terminals) and the variables of `src/style.css`.

Why. The text of every screen was one family and one size (`-apple-system`, 14 px), and only the terminals of Orca could be changed, through Ghostty's import (A-13). The owner wanted to choose them for each screen, and for the left list and the right work space of Todo and Orca apart (2026-11-08, Discussion #202, issue #203): plan B, revised: a tab in the settings, with no button in the side menu. The file editor of Orca (A-18) was added to the places by the owner's instruction (2026-11-08). The chat, the file pane, the tabs and the editor's heading of the Orca work pane (issue #385), the monospace family (#386) and the whole app (#387) were added by the owner's instruction (2026-11-10).

The places. A pick is a family and a size for one place:
- メール (the whole screen: the header, the list, a thread and its Orca pane), カレンダー (the whole screen) and Slack (the Slack list with its side menu of S-19, the history of a channel of S-20, a thread, the compose screen and the Claude pane of S-24): one pick each.
- Todo: プロジェクト一覧 (the list on the left, `#projects`) and 作業スペース (the right side: the header, the list, the detail and the Orca pane of A-9) apart.
- Orca: プロジェクト一覧 (`#orca-list`), 作業スペース（チャットとファイル）(`#orca-work` and `#orca-files` of the Orca screen: the tab strip, the file pane and the heading of the editor), 作業スペース（ターミナル）(the xterm.js terminals of the work pane) and エディタ (the text of the editor of A-18) apart. The Orca pane inside the mail and Todo screens follows the font of its screen.
- アプリ全体 (`app`): every part that no place above holds, that is the settings screen, the side menu, the popups and the tooltips. Its family is also the family of a place whose own family is 「システム標準」; its size reaches only the parts outside the places, and a place keeps its own size.
- 等幅の文字 (`mono`): the family only, with no size (see below). The text of an HTML mail body, the window title and the system's dialogs keep their own fonts (an HTML mail body that names none draws in Gmail's, M-2). The body of a text mail takes the family and the size of the mail screen, and Arial where none is chosen (M-2).

The card. The tab has one card, 「画面ごとのフォント」, in the shape of the other settings: the header row (the logo, the name and the `?` hint `#font-help`), and under it one row for each place, in the order of the list above (`#font-items`). A row has the name of the screen (`.item-name`), the place (`.item-meta`), a select of the family (`#font-{place}-family`) and a select of the size (`#font-{place}-size`), with `{place}` the area in kebab case (`app`, `mail`, `calendar`, `todo-list`, `todo-work`, `slack`, `orca-list`, `orca-pane`, `orca-work`, `editor`, `mono`). The rows are in the order アプリ全体 (`app`), メール, カレンダー, Todo (the list and the work space), Slack, Orca プロジェクト一覧, Orca 作業スペース（チャットとファイル）(`orca-pane`), Orca 作業スペース（ターミナル）, Orca エディタ and 等幅の文字 (`mono`, the last); the row of `mono` has the family select only (`#font-mono-size` does not exist), and its second text is 「書体のみ」. The family select starts with 「システム標準」 (the app's own: the sans-serif stack of the app, the monospace stack of the terminals and the editor, and, for the monospace text, the stacks as they are), and goes on with the families of the fonts on this Mac in alphabetical order, case ignored; only the select of the work space of Orca has 「Ghostty の設定に従う」 as its second entry. The size select has 8 px to 32 px in steps of 1 px, and, for a size held between two of them (the editor's 12.5), that one too. A family that is held but is not on this Mac stays in its select as 「{name}（この Mac にありません）」, so that drawing the card never changes the choice. While Ghostty's settings are followed, the size select of the work space of Orca is disabled. A select waits (is disabled) while a save is on its way, and has the focus again when it ends. The card shows no text about what the selects do (the `?` hint has it).

The size. The size of a place is the size of its ordinary text, in pixels, as 14 px is the app's own. The text in the place that sets its own size in `src/style.css` takes it in the same ratio: every `font-size` there is its pixels times the variable `--fs`, which is the size chosen divided by 14 (so 21 px makes the text that is 12 px on its own 18 px). The family is not changed for the text that names a family of its own (code, the monospace parts): those take the family of the place `mono` instead, and the size of the place they are in. The terminals and the editor are not scaled: they take the size itself (xterm.js `fontSize`; the text area's `font-size`).

Applying. At the start the app reads the saved choice and applies it before the settings are opened; a choice made in the card applies when Rust answers, and a failed save leaves the screens as they were. For the HTML screens `ts/font-settings.ts` sets `--ff-{place}` (the family, quoted, with the app's own after it) and `--fs-{place}` on `:root`, and removes them for the app's own family and for 14 px; `src/style.css` reads them on the root of each place. For the places of the Orca work pane the variables are `--ff-orca-pane` and `--fs-orca-pane`, read on `#orca-work` and `#orca-files`. For `app` they are `--ff-app` (the family, quoted, with `-apple-system, sans-serif` after it) and `--fs-app`: `:root` takes `--ff-base` from `--ff-app` (the family that every place falls back to with `var(--ff-{place}, var(--ff-base))`, so a place at 「システム標準」 follows it) and `--fs` from `--fs-app`, and `body` draws with them; a place sets its own `--fs` on its root, so `--fs-app` reaches the parts outside the places only. The settings screen's tabs wrap and its selects have a minimum height, so a large size does not push anything out of the window. For `mono` the variable is `--ff-mono`, the family quoted and followed by a comma, and every monospace stack of `src/style.css` starts with `var(--ff-mono, )` (the code of Markdown and of popups, the prompt, the diffs and the Slack code); with none chosen the variable is missing and every stack is as it was. The body of a text mail `.plain` is not a monospace stack: it takes `var(--ff-mail, Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif)`, the family chosen for the mail screen, else Gmail's (M-2). The color swatch of the terminal themes (A-14), the terminals and the editor's text are not touched. The terminals are drawn with the family (the app's own after it) and size chosen, with the weight normal, and fitted to their panes again; the choice 「Ghostty の設定に従う」 gives them Ghostty's import (A-13) as it was. The editor reads `--editor-family` and `--editor-size`. No font is loaded from outside, and the page has no list of fonts of its own: the families come from Rust.

The families. `font_families` answers the family names of the fonts on this Mac: CoreText's `CTFontManagerCopyAvailableFontFamilyNames` (`objc2-core-text`, the crate that Tauri's own dependencies already contain). The names the system hides (those that start with a dot) and the ones that cannot be used (see below) are left out; the rest are in alphabetical order, case ignored, once each. Other systems answer none. The list is read each time the app starts, and again by a save that names a family (outside the lock of the save, and not at all for a save without one); it is not saved.

The commands. `font_settings` answers the saved settings. `font_settings_set(area, family, size, ghostty)` takes one place (`app`, `mail`, `calendar`, `todoList`, `todoWork`, `slack`, `orcaList`, `orcaPane`, `orcaWork`, `editor` or `mono`; any other name is refused by the type), saves it and answers all the settings. `family` is null for the app's own; a name must be one that `font_families` answers, with the case of the system, or the family that the place holds already (so the size of a place, and the work space of Orca's choice of Ghostty, can still be saved after this Mac has lost the font); anything else is refused, in Japanese, with 「書体「{name}」はこの Mac にありません」. `size` is brought into 8 to 32 and to the nearest half pixel (the upper one from the middle); a size that is not a number is refused with 「大きさが正しくありません」. The place `mono` has no size: the size sent for it is not saved, and its pick keeps 14. `ghostty` may be true only for `orcaWork` (otherwise 「Ghostty の設定に従えるのは、Orca の作業スペースだけです」); the family and the size are kept then, for the day the choice is changed. A save reads the file, changes the one place and writes it again; two saves at once are made one after the other.

The file. `font-settings.json` in the app config directory (written through a temporary file and a rename): an object with a pick for each place, `{"app": {"family": null, "size": 14.0, "ghostty": false}, "mail": …, "calendar": …, "todoList": …, "todoWork": …, "slack": …, "orcaList": …, "orcaPane": …, "orcaWork": {"family": null, "size": 13.0, "ghostty": true}, "editor": {"family": null, "size": 12.5, "ghostty": false}, "mono": {"family": null, "size": 14.0, "ghostty": false}}`. The first values are 14 for the app and for every screen (the pane of Orca included), 13 and Ghostty's for the terminals and 12.5 for the editor, so that nothing looks different until a choice is made. A file written before the places `app`, `orcaPane` and `mono` were added has no key for them and reads with their first values; the `size` of `mono` read from a file is always 14. A file that is missing, is not JSON or is not an object gives the first values. A place that is missing or is not an object gives its first values; inside a pick, each field is mended on its own: a family that is not a string, is blank, has a control character or is over 200 characters becomes the app's own; a size that is not a number becomes the first size, and one out of the range is brought into it and to the half pixel; `ghostty` that is not true or false is the first value, and is false for every place but the work space of Orca. The family of a saved pick is not checked against the fonts on this Mac, so a font that is removed later does not lose the choice (the screen falls back to the app's own font while it is missing). A failed read of the settings (the config directory cannot be found) shows its message (`#error`) and draws no row; a failed read of the families shows its message and leaves the family selects with 「システム標準」 (and 「Ghostty の設定に従う」); a failed save shows its message, and the selects show the saved choice again.

Unit tests (`cargo test`) cover the first values (the app, the pane of Orca and the monospace text included), the old file without the three keys, the new places read from a file and mended, the size of `mono` that stays 14 whatever is sent or read, the size (the range, the half pixel, the middle going up, infinity), the family name (trim, blank, control characters, 200 characters not bytes), the list of families (hidden names, trim, order, case, duplicates), the file (saved and read back, its keys, a file that cannot be used, each field mended on its own, the work space that stops following Ghostty), the setting of a pick (the family checked against the installed ones and trimmed, the other places unchanged, a refused family, a size that is not a number, `ghostty` for the work space of Orca only, each area set in its own place, the area names, among them `app`, `orcaPane` and `mono`) and, on macOS, that the Mac has families to choose from; none touches the real config folder. The E2E spec `settings.e2e.ts` covers the tab (its place among the eight, the eleven rows and their order with no size select for the monospace text, the families offered and Ghostty's only for the work space of Orca, the sizes and the half size, the saved choice shown, a family this Mac lacks, the choice applied at the start, the family and the size sent and drawn at once, the list and the work space of Todo apart, the places of Todo, Slack and Orca, the way back to the system's font, following Ghostty and stopping, the editor's variables, the whole app's family reaching the places at the system's font and its size reaching only the parts outside them, the settings kept inside the window at 32 px, the pane of Orca's variables, the monospace family put in front of the stacks (the body of a text mail taking the family of the mail screen and not the monospace one) and the first values putting no variable, a failed save, a failed read of the settings and of the families, and the hint), and `orca.e2e.ts` covers the terminals (the font chosen, Ghostty's kept while it is followed, a font chosen in the settings drawn with no restart, and the project list) and the work pane (the file pane drawn with the family and size chosen for it, apart from the list, and with the family of the whole app when none is chosen), and `orca-files.e2e.ts` covers the editor (the font chosen and the first one, and its heading drawn with the font of the work pane).

## A-23 No title bar

The window has no title bar: every screen reaches the top edge of the window, and the traffic lights (close, minimize, zoom) of macOS sit on the top of the green icon column (`#sidebar`). The window is set in `app.windows` of `src-tauri/tauri.conf.json` (`titleBarStyle` `Overlay`, `hiddenTitle` true and `trafficLightPosition`), the permission to move the window is `src-tauri/capabilities/default.json`, and the frontend is `src/index.html` and `src/style.css`.

Why. The gray title bar took a band of height off every screen and showed a title (cookapps) the owner does not need. The owner asked for the screens to fill the window to the top (issue #314, part of #222; Discussion #221).

The icon column. The traffic lights are at the top of `#sidebar`, at `trafficLightPosition` x 9 and y 20 (the column is 78px wide, and the three buttons are 60pt wide, 9pt from each side; measured on macOS 27.2.1, where macOS puts the close button at x 9 itself, and x 6 had put the edge of the window over the buttons, #421), and the icons start 48px from the top, below them. Apart from the width of the column, which moves the screens to its right by the same amount, nothing else moves: the header of the list, the reload of the Orca screen and the bands of the other screens keep their places.

The bands to drag the window by. The band at the top of each screen carries `data-tauri-drag-region`, so pressing an empty place of it and dragging moves the window, and a double press zooms it as the title bar did. The bands are the header of the mail list (`#inbox-header`) and of a thread (`#mail-header`), the bar of the calendar (`#cal-bar`), the header of the Todo screen (`#todo-body > header`) and the row of icons over its right side (`#todo-tools`, T-7), the heading and tabs of the settings (`#settings-head`), the header of the Slack list (`#slack-header`), of a Slack thread (`#st-head`), of the Slack compose screen and of the Todo prompt screen, the header of the Orca list (`#orca-header`), the bar of each pane of the work pane (`.orca-tabbar`, set in `ts/orca-pane-tree.ts`), the bar of the file pane (`#orca-files-bar`), and `#sidebar` itself. The value is `deep` on a band with a title (a press on its text moves the window) and bare on a band whose empty place is the band itself. A button, a link, a field, and an element with a `tabindex` or a role of a control still take the press (Tauri's own rule), so nothing in a band changes what it does.

The height of the bands (issue #482). Every band above, except the heading and tabs of the settings (`#settings-head`, which is not a band of one row), is as high as `--header-h` of `:root` (40px at the default font, and stretching to fit its text with a larger font, A-20: `--header-h` is a minimum height, and the tab bar `.orca-tabbar` is at least 32px in proportion to the font of the work pane), and an icon button in one is `--header-btn` (24px) with an icon of `--header-ic` (15px); no screen sets a value of its own. The header of a thread (`#mail-header`) holds "戻る" only: the operations of the mail are in a row above the subject, not in a band, so they are 34px with an icon of 20px (M-2, issue #503). The tab row of the right pane of a Slack thread (`#reply-chat .chat-head`, with the Claude and Orca tabs) is a band too, so that its line meets `#st-head`. The second row of the Todo's right side (`.chat-head` and `.ho-head`, 48px, T-7) and the heading of the file pane (`#orca-files-head`) are headings of a pane under a band, not bands, and keep their own heights. The traffic lights sit in `#sidebar`, not in a band, so the height changes neither them nor the bands to drag by. The list button of the Todo's `#projects` is centered on the height of the band beside it. E2E (`orca.e2e.ts`, `todo-claude.e2e.ts`) checks the heights of `#orca-header`, `.orca-tabbar`, `#todo-body > header` and `#todo-tools` and the size of the buttons.

The list of projects of the Orca screen can be closed (issue #318, part of #222). A button, `.icon-btn.list-toggle` with the icon of a window with a side column (`SIDEBAR_SVG` in `ts/icons.ts`; made by `listToggle` in `ts/list-toggle.ts`), has `aria-label` 「プロジェクト一覧」 and says by `aria-expanded` whether the list is open, so that the Todo screen's list can take the same button. While the list is open, the button is the first in the list's header (`#orca-header`), at its left end. Pressing it hides the list (`#orca-list`) and its divider (`#orca-split`), and the work pane widens to take the width; the button then appears at the left end of the tab strip of the pane at the left edge of the work pane (the first pane of a row, the first of a column, down to a pane), and pressing it opens the list again. The button is at the same x in both places, since the list's header and the work pane both start at the right edge of the green column and the button has the same inset from it (issue #570: #437 had put it in the tab strip in both states, and the owner chose this placement again, because the button then moved with the list's width when the list was opened). The press hides the button that was pressed, so the focus goes on to the button that is shown. The green icon column (`#sidebar`) is never closed. The width the divider had is kept in memory, so the list opens at the width it had before; the state is not saved in any file, and the screen is open again when the app starts. ⌘J (the search, A-19) opens a closed list first. The button is a button of a band, so a press on it does not move the window.

The list of projects of the Todo screen can be closed the same way (issue #322, part of #222). The button is the same control (`listToggle`, same icon, `aria-label` 「プロジェクト一覧」, `aria-expanded`). While the list (`#projects`) is open, the button (`#projects-toggle`) is the first child of the list, at its top left, and it is kept when the list of projects is drawn again. Pressing it hides the list, and the task list (`#todo-body`) widens to take its width; the button then appears (`#todo-list-toggle`) as the first of the header (`#todo-body > header`), at the left of the heading (the back button, when it shows, comes after it), and pressing it opens the list again. The list has no divider, so none is hidden with it. The token form has no list, so it shows neither button, and the choice comes back after it. The green icon column (`#sidebar`) is never closed. The state is held in memory only: it stays while the screen is left and shown again, and the screen is open again when the app starts. The drag of a task or of a subtask onto a project (T-2, T-6) works as before while the list is open. The calendar screen uses the same control for the panel of its calendars (C-4), and the mail, Slack and settings screens have no such button.

The permission. A drag region calls the window plugin's `start_dragging` (and `internal_toggle_maximize` for the double press), which Tauri v2 lets a window use only through a capability. `capabilities/default.json` gives the window `main` `core:window:allow-start-dragging` and `core:window:allow-internal-toggle-maximize`, and no other permission. The E2E build names its own capability in `tauri.e2e.conf.json`, which replaces this one.

Checked against. The names and the shape of `titleBarStyle`, `hiddenTitle` and `trafficLightPosition`, the values of `data-tauri-drag-region` and the commands it calls are in `tauri-utils` 2.10.2 (`config.rs`), and `tauri` 2.12.5 (`src/window/scripts/drag.js`, `permissions/window/autogenerated/reference.md`). How `x` and `y` place the buttons was read in `tao` 0.37.2 (`inset_traffic_lights`: the close button goes to `x`), and the size and the standard position of the buttons (14pt, 23pt apart, close at x 9 and 9pt from the top) were measured on macOS 27.2.1 with a test window of AppKit (#421); the height of the buttons can differ between versions of macOS, so the 48px under them was not checked by eye.

E2E (`sidebar.e2e.ts` and the spec of each screen) checks that each band has the attribute and that a button in it has none, that a press on a button of a band (the back button of a thread, a tab of the settings) still works, and that the first icon of the column starts at least 40px from the top. `orca.e2e.ts` also checks the button of the list: it closes and opens the list (the divider and the width of the work pane with it), the button is at the left end of the header while the list is open (and not in the tab strip of the left pane), and at the left end of the tab strip of the left pane while it is closed (and not in the header), the two left edges are at the same x (within 1px), the focus stays on the button that is shown after a press, the width is the same after it opens, and the state is not kept after the screen is reloaded. `todo.e2e.ts` checks the button of the Todo screen the same way (in the list, then in the header at the left of the heading, the tasks taking the list's width, the focus, the state kept while the screen is left, a drop on a project after the list was opened again), and `sidebar.e2e.ts` that the mail, Slack and settings screens show none and the calendar screen shows the panel's button alone. The move of the window itself is not checked: the E2E window is not shown, and the drag is native.

## A-28 The app's log

The app keeps a log of what happened in a file of the config directory, and the screens and Rust both write to it and read it. A line has a time, a weight (`error`, `warn` or `info`), a place and a few words of text. This part shows nothing on a screen: the card at the bottom right that shows a failure and the 「ログ」 tab of the settings read and write it in later parts (#431, #430). The code is `src-tauri/src/app_log.rs`; the commands are `log_write` and `log_read`.

Why. A failure was shown under the header (`#error`) with no weight and no trace of it afterwards. The owner decided (2026-11-11, issue #428) that what is not critical is kept in a log and read later. This is the first of three parts (issue #432).

The file. `app-log.jsonl` in the app config directory, one JSON object a line, the oldest first: `time` (UTC, RFC 3339 with milliseconds, such as `2026-11-14T03:04:05.678Z`), `level`, `source` (where it happened: a command, a module or a screen, as the caller names it) and `message`. It is written by appending, and by a rewrite through a temporary file (`write_atomic`) when old lines are dropped. A line that is not an entry, or whose time is not a time, is left out of a read and dropped at the next rewrite.

What goes in. Only what the caller says happened, in a few words. A token, a mail, a Slack message, a Todo, what a terminal printed and what was typed are never written; the callers must not pass them (the contract, written on `app_log::write`). The module backs this up and does not replace it: the text is cut to 500 characters (`…` at the cut) and the place to 64, a line break or a hidden character (control, zero-width, bidirectional) becomes a space, and a word shaped like a secret is masked as `***` before the cut: a token of a known service (`xoxb-`, `xoxp-`, `xapp-` and the other Slack kinds, `ya29.`, `1//` (a Google refresh token), `sk-ant-`, `ghp_`, `github_pat_`, `AIza`), a run of 32 or more letters, digits, `_` and `-` that holds a digit (the token of a service not in the list, such as a Todoist token of 40 hexadecimal digits; a `.`, `/` or `:` ends a run, so a path or an address is not one, and a long word without a digit stays), the value after `token`, `secret`, `password`, `passwd`, `api_key`, `apikey` or `authorization` followed by `=` or `:` (the next word when the value is apart, the next two for `authorization`), and the word after `Bearer`. A text that is empty once cleaned is refused (an error in Japanese) and nothing is written; an empty place is written as `-`.

What is kept. Lines older than 30 days are dropped, and the file is kept at 1 MiB or under (the newest lines stay). A write that makes the file pass 1 MiB cuts it down to 90% of that, so the next write does not cut it again. A write also reads the file to drop old lines the first time in a run and then at most once an hour. A read leaves out the lines older than 30 days at once, whether or not the file was rewritten. A missing file is an empty log.

The commands. `log_write(level, source, message)` appends a line (answers null) and `log_read` answers the lines, the newest first. Rust code calls `app_log::write(dir, level, source, message)` with the config directory (`&Path`), as the other modules do. Writes and rewrites hold one lock, so two at once do not meet. A write that fails answers an error and logs nowhere else, so a failing log cannot make a loop.

No new setting, no new call to an outside service, no new Tauri permission. No screen text is added (`frontend.md`).

Unit tests (`cargo test`) are those of `app_log.rs`: the cleaning of the text (spaces, the cut, the mask of each kind of secret and words that only mention one), the entry (time, place, an empty text), the line (one line of JSON that reads back, bad lines left out), the dropping of old lines and the size cap, the newest-first read, and a write, read and cap against a temporary folder. No E2E spec: no screen calls the commands yet.
