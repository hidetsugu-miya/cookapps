# Mail requirements

## M-1 List

Show the threads in the inbox, split into unread and read, each sorted newest first. The unread rows have a heading above them (`#unread-heading`, in the scrolling area, the pair of the heading "既読" above the read rows): "未読" and the number of unread threads of the list at the left and M-6's "すべて既読にする" at the right; with no unread thread the heading is not shown. The list has a header (`#inbox-header`, fixed above the list in the tint of the thread bar, shown on the list only: the title "受信トレイ" only) and no reload button except the one of a failed load below (the bar `#mail-header` is shown only in a thread). Above the rows, `#inbox-state` (in the scrolling area, hidden unless the list is shown) shows the state of the list in three ways: while the first load has not answered and no row is drawn, a spinner with "読み込んでいます…" (the list is shown at startup for this; no list kept by an earlier run is drawn, and nothing but the spinner and the note is shown until the first load answers, #739); when a load answered and there is no row, "受信トレイにスレッドはありません"; and when the last load failed (the call, or an account in its answer), one line "読み込めませんでした: {the error, or each failed account and its error}。自動でも読み直します" with the button "再読み込み", the only time the button is there. The button loads the list at once, and it cannot be pressed while a load is on its way; when rows are drawn they stay under the line. The failure belongs to the list (and is also written to the log, A-28) and not to a card, so it stays when another screen is shown and the list comes back, and it goes when a load succeeds with no failed account; only the latest load decides it. A load that failed (the call, or an account in its answer) is repeated by the sync round, which runs every 10 seconds and when the window is shown again, once the wait has passed since that load (60 seconds, doubled after each failed repeat up to 5 minutes), until a load succeeds with no failed account, which clears the wait; when a sync answers that an account needs a login while the list is not shown (a thread is open), the list is loaded again at the next sync round; a load never switches the screen; a repeated load keeps its failure for the list whichever screen is shown; a load that draws the rows again keeps the focus on the same button of the same row

## M-2 Read

Open a thread and show its body. Pressing a row of the list (or "詳細を開く" of a row of the pane of the unread, A-35, or a task's mail link) goes to the detail view at once and shows the subject and the sender that the row has (a thread opened from a task with no row shows neither), and in the body's place the note "読み込んでいます…" with gray lines; when the thread arrives they are replaced by the messages. Until it arrives, the buttons of the row of operations (below: the AI summary, Slack, Todo, trash and archive, and the icon of the pane to Orca on the right side, M-10) cannot be pressed, and "戻る" of the header goes back to where the thread was opened from. A press on another row or on "戻る" while it loads leaves the earlier answer ignored: it never draws over the screen then shown. When the call fails, the same error text as before is shown in the body's place (and the buttons stay disabled). When the answer says the account must log in again, the list is shown. A thread that was opened before in this run is drawn at once from the copy the app holds of Gmail's last answer for it, with no note and with the buttons enabled as for a thread that arrived, and Gmail is asked again at the same time. When the answer draws the same (the subject, the messages and their images), nothing is drawn again, so a body keeps its place and its frames do not load again; when it differs (a new reply, say), the thread is drawn again from the answer. When that call fails, the copy stays readable and the error goes to the log (A-28) only, not in the body's place and not on a card; when the answer says the account must log in again, the list is shown as above. Whether the thread is marked read (M-6) follows Gmail's answer, not the copy. The copies are in memory only, never in a file, and are gone when the app quits; they take at most 64 MB (`HELD_THREAD_BYTES`), the oldest dropped first, and a thread larger than that is not held. The copies of an account are dropped when the account is removed, and when the fetch of one of its threads (`thread` or `thread_refresh`) gets the answer that the account must log in again. A thread that is not held is waited for as described above. Why. The owner asked for a faster mail screen: every open, the reopen of the same thread included, waited for Gmail behind the note (issue #614). The header of the open thread (`#mail-header`, as high as every band, A-23) has "戻る" (`#back`) only. The operations of the open thread are a row of icon buttons (`#thread-actions`, the first child of `#thread`, just above the subject) in this order: the AI summary (`#thread-summary`, shown only while Claude can be used), "Slack に貼る" (`#thread-slack`), "Todo にする" (`#thread-todo`), and "ゴミ箱へ移動" (`#trash`) and "アーカイブ" (`#archive`). They are in three groups set apart by a thin vertical line: the summary; "Slack に貼る" and "Todo にする"; and the trash and the archive (a line is not drawn where the group before it, or the group itself, is hidden: no summary while Claude cannot be used, no trash and archive for a thread opened from a task with no row). The row is 48px high with a line under it (the owner's instruction, 2026-11-12, issue #488). Each is a `.icon-btn` of 34px with an icon of 20px, with its `aria-label` as the tooltip, and the buttons sit at the row's right end, the right end of the body (the owner's instruction, 2026-11-13, issue #529). The row stays at the top of the scrolling area (`position: sticky`) while the messages scroll, in the color of the body so that nothing shows through it, and it is within the area at the left of the right side (M-10). The reply field of M-10 is fixed at the bottom of the same area. Why. The owner chose this layout (issue #503, option A): the buttons stayed far from the subject, at the right end of the bar, and the bar is now only "戻る". The owner then asked for the buttons at the right end of their own row (issue #529). The body is drawn as Gmail draws it. An HTML body that names no font draws in `Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif` at 13px: a rule of the frame's root and body ahead of the mail's own styles, so a font the mail names (its CSS, an inline style, `<font face>`) wins, and so does a size it names on the body or inside it (a size named only on the root does not, because the body's 13px is set under it). A text body (`.plain`) draws in the same sans-serif, not a monospace one, at 13px; with a font chosen for the mail screen (A-20) it takes that family and size, while an HTML body keeps its own fonts (A-20). Line breaks and wrapping of a text body are as they were. The http(s) URL that stands as text in an HTML body, outside a link, is a link too: the text nodes of the frame's document (not those inside `a`, `script`, `style`, `textarea`, `title`, `svg`, `math`, `select`, `button`, `noframes`, `noembed`, `xmp`, `plaintext`) are searched for URLs by the rule of a text body (a URL ends at whitespace, a non-ASCII character or `"` `<` `>`, trailing punctuation is left out, and a trailing `)` only when unpaired), and each is wrapped in an `<a href>` built with DOM calls (the mail's text is never put in as markup). It then goes the way of every link (`routeLinks`: `target="_top"` and the URL as the title, opened in the default browser by Rust), and nothing else of the frame's sandbox and CSP changes. Text that is not an http(s) URL is left as text. A URL that another element cuts (a `<wbr>`, a `<span>`) is a link up to the cut, because each text node is searched on its own. Why. The owner reported that a mail drew in a face unlike Gmail's, and that a URL in the text of an HTML body could not be pressed (issues #494 and #495, parts of #493).

## M-6 Mark read

Mark a thread read or unread with the checkbox in the list. The button "すべて既読にする" at the right end of the heading "未読" above the unread rows (M-1; the heading, and so the button, is not shown while no thread is unread; the button is disabled while a run is on its way) makes every unread thread in the list (the ones shown, not unread threads that are not on the screen) read with the existing `set_read`, one request per thread, at most 4 at once: the rows change first, a row whose request fails (or answers that the account must log in again) goes back to unread and the failures are reported once, on a card (A-28), and a read box stays disabled while its request is on its way so that a sync leaves the row as it is (as for a single toggle). When it ends, the notice "N 件を既読にしました" offers "元に戻す", which makes the threads it changed (those still in the list) unread again the same way

## M-7 Auto sync

Sync the inbox with Gmail about every 10 seconds. A round that fails shows nothing on the screen: the third round in a row that fails writes one line to the log (A-28), 「自動同期に失敗しています: …」, and the count starts again when a round works

## M-8 AI summary

In the mail detail, an icon summarizes the thread with the Claude Code CLI (`claude -p`, the model and effort of A-2 with sonnet and medium as the default, no tools, no MCP, no settings) and shows a short list of points, in Japanese, on a card under the subject. Only pressing the icon sends the subject and, of each message, the sender, the date and the text (newest first, up to 10,000 characters, the oldest dropped first). The summary is kept in memory per thread (and its latest message), never saved, and shown again without sending when the card is closed and reopened. The icon is shown only while Claude can be used (logged in, or an API key that is not refused, A-4)

## M-10 Reply field

In the open thread (M-2) the head of the latest message, the first one in the list, which is open, has a reply button (`#mail-reply-open`, an icon button with the arrow of a reply and the label "返信") at the right of its date. No other message has one. Pressing it shows the reply field under the messages, at the bottom of the area at the left of the right side (below), and puts the cursor in its text (once Milkdown is read, see below); the message does not fold. Until it is pressed the field is hidden: it is hidden when the thread has arrived (not under the note of M-2, and not on the list), and it is empty for each thread that is opened: leaving a thread, or opening another, drops what was written and hides the field.
The shown field (`#mail-reply`) takes half the height of `#main`, which holds the thread to the height of the area, and is `position: sticky` at the bottom of it, so the messages scroll above it, and the row of operations stays at the top; at the end of the scroll the last message is seen above the field. The text, with the toolbar over it, fills the height that the rows and the bottom line leave, and the text scrolls inside it; the height cannot be changed by hand. When Gmail's answer draws the thread again (M-2), the field stays as it is, shown, with the text and the recipients kept. The field holds, from the top:

- The kind of reply (`#mail-reply-mode`, a select): "返信" (the default) or "全員に返信". The recipients are chips (`.mail-reply-chip`: the name, else the address, with × to take it off; the address too, as `Name <address>`, for a Reply-To whose address is none of the From's, so that the chip shows where the reply goes) of To, then the words "Cc" and "Bcc" at the right. Pressing one opens its row (`#mail-reply-cc-row`, `#mail-reply-bcc-row`), with its chips and a field to add one (an address typed in it becomes a chip with Enter, with a comma (read from the text of the field, so a comma pasted or inserted by an input method counts too) or by leaving the field; Rust checks the address when the mail is sent). A row with a chip is open.
- The text (`#mail-reply-body`) under them: the rich editor of S-21 (Milkdown, A-26), made by `rich-editor.ts` with the prefix `mail-reply-body` and with no send arrow, one frame with the toolbar of S-21 (`#mail-reply-body-tools`, the same nine buttons and link field), and the field (`#mail-reply-body-input`, the editable part `.ProseMirror` with `aria-label` "返信の本文", with no placeholder and no line of text that explains it), which takes all the height under the toolbar. Milkdown is read the first time the reply button opens the field (not when the thread is drawn); until it is there the toolbar and the field are off, and then the cursor is put in the text. A read that fails shows "エディターを読み込めませんでした: …" on a card (A-28), and the next press of the reply button reads it again. Enter is a line break, and Cmd+Enter (Ctrl+Enter off macOS, unless it only confirms an IME conversion) sends, as "送信" does. While a mail is going out the toolbar and the field wait; if the thread is left and opened again meanwhile, the field of that thread can be written in.
- The files (M-12): the strip of chips (`#mail-reply-attach`) under the text, hidden while there is none.
- "送信" (`#mail-reply-send`), at the left of the bottom line, the clip of M-12 (`#mail-reply-attach-open`) right of it, and "破棄" (`#mail-reply-discard`), at the right. "送信" cannot be pressed while the text is blank (spaces and line breaks alone) or while a mail is going out, and Cmd+Enter sends nothing then either.

Who a reply goes to is worked out by Rust (`reply_choices` in `mail_address.rs`; the thread's `reply`), from the headers of the last message of the thread that is not a draft, the one the reply is threaded to (`send_mail`): "返信" goes to its Reply-To, else its From, and "全員に返信" to that and to the other people in its To, with its Cc in Cc. Each address is listed once (the case of the address is ignored), and the account that reads the thread is never listed, unless it is the only address the message went to. A message that the account sent is answered to the people it was sent to. A Reply-To whose address is none of the From's is the one case where the chip's label is the name and the address (`Name <address>`; with no name the address alone), decided in `reply_choices`. A known limit: whether a message was sent by the account is decided by the account's own address only, so a reply to a mail that the account sent from one of its send-as aliases goes back to that alias (the list of aliases would need a new call to Gmail's API, which is not made). Choosing the kind fills To and Cc again (what was typed in them goes, and Bcc stays). Leaving the thread ("戻る", the trash or the archive) while a reply is going out empties and hides the field at once (as it does when no reply is going out); when the send ends, the thread is not read again and nothing is drawn.

"送信" calls `send_mail` (M-5: the account that received the thread is the sender) with the chips' addresses as To, Cc and Bcc, the thread's subject, the Markdown that the editor exports as `body` (it ends with a line end) with `markdown` true, and the thread's ID as `replyThreadId` (Rust adds "Re: " and the threading headers). With `markdown` true Rust sends a `multipart/alternative` mail (`mail_send.rs`): the first part is `text/plain` holding the Markdown as it is, and the second is `text/html` made from it (`markdown_to_html`: paragraphs, headings, quotes, lists, code, emphasis, strong, strikethrough and links; a raw HTML tag in the text is written out as text, not passed on as a tag, except that the empty paragraph the editor writes as `<br />` alone in its block is a blank paragraph (`<p><br></p>`) in the HTML and is left out of the `text/plain` Markdown, which leaves one more blank line there; only `http`, `https` and `mailto` links keep their anchor; an image is left as its alt text), both as UTF-8 base64. Without `markdown` (a new mail, M-11) the mail stays one `text/plain` part. With files (M-12) the text goes first inside a `multipart/mixed`. When it succeeds, the field is emptied (the text, Cc and Bcc, and the recipients of "返信") and hidden, and the thread is read again (`thread`) and drawn with the sent mail in it. When it fails, the error is shown on a card (A-28) and the field stays shown, with the text and the recipients as they were. When the answer says the account must log in again, the list is shown at the next load as for any command (M-2) and the text stays. "破棄" empties the field in the same way and hides it, with no call.

The right side (#490; the owner's decision of 2026-11-12, issue #486 and #490: the pane at the right is not a tab, and it is switched by icons as the Todo screen's is, T-7; the layout is the artifact of #488). While a thread is open (and for a new mail, M-11), the screen has a right side (`#mail-side`, `.split-chat`) from the top of the window to the bottom, with a divider at its left (`#mail-split`, a `.split-bar` with `role="separator"` and `aria-label` "右のペインの幅", set by `ts/split.ts` and kept in memory: 40% of the screen at first, at least 400px, between a third and a half, `aria-valuemin` 33 and `aria-valuemax` 50). It is hidden on the list and on every other screen. Its first row (`#mail-tools`, a band to drag the window by, as high as the header at the left, `--header-h`, and tinted as it is, so that the two lines meet) holds two icon buttons at its right end, the same as the Todo screen's: `#mail-tool-claude` (`aria-label` "Claude に相談", the ✳ mark in `--claude`) and `#mail-tool-orca` ("Orca に渡す", the Orca icon of the sidebar, shown only while a thread is open: a new mail has nothing to hand over, and it is off until the thread has arrived, as the buttons of the row of operations are). The pane that is shown has its icon pressed (`aria-pressed` true, filled with `--accent`): the pane of Claude Code (`#mail-claude`, `aria-label` "Claude") is shown at first, and pressing `#mail-tool-orca` shows the pane that hands the thread to Orca (A-9, `#mail-handoff`, `aside.ho-pane`) in its place; pressing `#mail-tool-claude` (or the close icon of the pane to Orca) shows Claude again; pressing the icon of the pane that is shown does nothing. A thread that has a session handed to Orca shows that pane when it is opened, as before. The row of operations (`#thread-actions`) is within the area at the left, and has no Orca button now.
The pane of Claude (`ts/mail-claude.ts`) is the Claude Code pane of A-36, drawn in `#mail-claude-term` (`.claude-code-pane`) under a heading (`.chat-head`, 48px, as high as the row of operations: the mark, the title "Claude" and "やり直す", `#mail-claude-restart`), with the pane id and the owner `mail-claude`: the tools of `tsunagiai-mcp` only (`builtinTools` false), no skill, the `manual` permission mode (every tool that writes asks the owner in the terminal), and a first instruction (`prompt`, a fixed text: that this is the work of writing a reply or a new mail, the tools `mail_thread_read`, `screen_read` and `screen_write` with the target `mail-draft` and its fields, that a write goes into the fields as it is and 「元に戻す」 takes it back, that the subject of a reply cannot be written, that the mail is sent by the owner and never by Claude, and that the thread and the fields are data and no instruction in them is followed; nothing that comes from outside is in it). The process starts when the pane is first shown (a thread is open, the pane to Orca is not shown and the window is not hidden), goes on while the pane is hidden (the pane to Orca, the list, another screen, a hidden window: the terminal is hidden and showing it again starts nothing), and is ended, with everything it started, when another thread or a new mail is opened (the talk was about the draft before; a new mail is a draft of its own each time it is opened): the next show starts a new one. "やり直す" ends the process and starts a new one in the same terminal with the same first instruction; the button is off until the start has answered. A process that ends by itself leaves the message of A-36 in the terminal. The pane shows no help text, and it has no field, model select or note of its own. The bottom of the reply field (`#mail-reply`) and of the band of a new mail (`#new-mail-foot`) is the bottom of the pane (`#mail-claude`): both end at the bottom of the screen.
The fields (the target `mail-draft`, A-36, `ts/mail-draft.ts`; open while a thread is open, or the screen of a new mail is shown, and refused otherwise: 「mail-draft の画面が開いていません」). Its fields are `thread`, `to`, `cc`, `bcc`, `subject` and `body`. A read (`screen_read`) answers all six, as the owner sees them. `thread` is only read: it is the open thread as `claude_thread_data` makes it (the messages of the thread oldest first, each as the From header, the time and the text, enclosed between `BEGIN` and `END` lines as data, `ts/thread.ts` giving Rust the messages from the newest back until their text reaches the 10,000 characters Rust reads), a fixed line while a thread is not drawn yet or for a new mail (「新規作成です。読むスレッドはありません」); the tool `mail_thread_read` (A-36) answers it as it is. `to`, `cc` and `bcc` are the addresses of the chips, separated by a comma and a space (an address typed in a field and not yet a chip is made a chip first); `subject` is the thread's subject for a reply and the field's text for a new mail; `body` is the Markdown of the reply's editor (it ends with a line end, as the editor exports it, and is empty while the editor is not read) or the text of the new mail's field. A write (`screen_write`) puts the texts of the fields it names into the fields as they are, and the fields it leaves out stay: `to`, `cc` and `bcc` replace the chips of that row with the addresses of the text (separated by commas, semicolons or line breaks that are not inside double quotes or angle brackets; blank ones are left out; an address that a chip showed keeps that chip's label), and open the Cc or Bcc row for them; `body` replaces the text; `subject` replaces the subject of a new mail. It sends nothing and saves nothing: only "送信" sends. A write to a reply shows the reply field, as the reply button does, without moving the cursor, and reads Milkdown first when it is not read yet (a failure is the refusal 「エディターを読み込めませんでした: …」). Refused, in Japanese, with nothing written: the field `thread` (「読むだけ」), the subject of a reply (「件名のまま」), a field that the target does not have (「欄 … はありません」), a write while a mail is going out (「送信の最中のため、書けません」) and a write when neither is open. ## M-11 New mail

The header of the inbox (`#inbox-header`) has "作成" (`#mail-compose`, an icon button with a pencil). Pressing it opens the screen that writes a new mail in the area of the detail view (M-2): `#new-mail` takes the place of the thread in `#main`, and it is not a dialog. The header of the list gives way to a header of its own (`#new-mail-header`, a band like `#mail-header`, A-23) with "戻る" (`#new-mail-back`), the title "新規メッセージ" and "破棄" (`#new-mail-discard`) at the right. The screen is empty each time it is opened. It holds, from the top:

- "差出人": the account the mail goes from (M-5). With one account it is only shown (`#new-mail-from-text`); with several it is a select (`#new-mail-from-select`) of them, the first being chosen when the screen opens.
- "宛先", with the words "Cc" and "Bcc" at the right, and the rows of Cc and Bcc that they open (`#new-mail-cc-row`, `#new-mail-bcc-row`): the recipients as chips, made and opened as those of the reply field (M-10) are, by the same code (`ts/mail-recipients.ts`): an address typed in the field becomes a chip with Enter, a comma or by leaving the field, × takes a chip off, and Rust checks the addresses when the mail is sent. The chips start empty.
- "件名" (`#new-mail-subject`), and the text (`#new-mail-body`, plain text) under the rows, which takes the height that is left.
- The files (M-12): the strip of chips (`#new-mail-attach`) under the text, hidden while there is none.
- "送信" (`#new-mail-send`) in a band fixed at the bottom of the area (as `#mail-reply` is, M-10), with the clip of M-12 (`#new-mail-attach-open`) right of it. It cannot be pressed while the text is blank or while a mail is going out.

The screen shows no placeholder and no line that explains a field.

"送信" calls `send_mail` (M-10) with the chosen account, the chips' addresses as To, Cc and Bcc, the subject and the text, and no `replyThreadId` and no `markdown` (the mail is plain text). When it succeeds, the notice "送信しました" is shown and the inbox is shown again. When it fails, the error is shown on a card (A-28) and what was written stays. When the answer says the account must log in again, the list is shown at the next load as for any command (M-2) and what was written stays. A mail that goes out after the screen was left (by the sidebar, say) shows the notice and changes nothing else.

"戻る" and "破棄" show the inbox again and drop what was written, with no call. While the screen is shown, a load or a sync of the list does not draw the list over it (the sidebar still shows the inbox).

The right side of M-10 (#490) is at the right of the screen too, with the pane of Claude Code (`mail-claude`) and the fields of the target `mail-draft`, and without the pane to Orca (a new mail has nothing to hand over: `#mail-tool-orca` is not shown). A new mail is a draft of its own each time the screen is opened, so the process of Claude Code of an earlier thread or new mail ends and a new one starts. The five fields (`to`, `cc`, `bcc`, `subject` and `body`, here the plain text of `#new-mail-body`) are read and written as M-10 says, with 「元に戻す」 (`#new-mail-undo`) in the band at the bottom, right of "送信"; "戻る", "破棄" and leaving the screen forget the ways back. A write is refused while the mail goes out. The sender is the account the screen chose, and is not a field. Claude Code never sends: "送信" is the owner's.

Why. The owner chose the layout of issue #486, option B (the "send" in a band fixed at the bottom, as the reply field). The right side and the fields that Claude Code writes are those of M-10 (issue #490). Attaching files is M-12.

## M-12 Attach files

A reply (M-10) and a new mail (M-11) can carry files. A file is attached in three ways, and all three end in the same chip:

- The clip (`#mail-reply-attach-open` in the foot of the reply, `#new-mail-attach-open` in the band of a new mail, `.icon-btn`, `aria-label` "ファイルを添付", right of "送信"). Pressing it calls `mail_attach_pick`: Rust opens the dialog that picks files (`tauri-plugin-dialog`, one or several), reads the files the owner picked, and answers each as its name, its size and its bytes as base64; an empty list when the dialog was cancelled. The place of the files is the dialog's alone: the page gives no path, so the page cannot make Rust read a file of its own choosing.
- A drop on the field (`#mail-reply`, `#new-mail`), which the page takes as `File` objects (the window's own file drop is off, `ts/orca-attach.ts`), and a paste in the field or its text (a paste with no file is left alone, so the text pastes as before; a file copied in Finder is a file). The page asks Rust first, with `mail_attach_check(name, size, held)`, and reads the file only when Rust accepts it; a file with no name is called "file".
- The files of one take are added one after another, each checked against the ones before it.

The chips (`#mail-reply-attach`, between the text and the foot; `#new-mail-attach`, between the text and the band; hidden while there is no file) show for each file, in the order they were attached, its name, its size ("980 B", "12 KB", "1.5 MB") and a × (`aria-label` "{name} を外す") that takes it off. A file that Rust refuses is not attached, and the refusal is shown on a card (A-28) as it is (a Japanese message); the rest of the take stops. The screen shows no help text (`frontend.md`).

What Rust refuses (`check_attachment` in `mail_send.rs`, at the check of a file and again when the mail is sent): a name that is blank or longer than 255 bytes, a file with no byte, and a total of the files of the mail over 25 MB (25 × 1024 × 1024 bytes, Gmail's limit: 「添付の合計は 25 MB までです」). A file taken off frees its share.

The files are held in memory only: the page keeps each as base64 until the mail goes out or the field is let go, and Rust keeps none (`mail_attach_pick` and `mail_attach_check` store nothing). No file, no name and no byte of them is written to a file or to the log. The files are let go when the reply is sent, discarded ("破棄"), or its thread is left or another is opened, and when a new mail is sent, "戻る" or "破棄" is pressed, or the screen is opened again. A mail that fails to send, or whose account must log in again, keeps them. While a mail goes out, the clip and the × are off and a drop or a paste adds nothing; a read that ends after its mail was let go adds nothing.

"送信" calls `send_mail` with `attachments`, a list of `{name, data}` (the name as the file was named, the data as base64) in the order of the chips, left out when there is no file (so a mail with no file is the call of M-10 and M-11 as it was). "送信" is still off while the text is blank: files alone do not send a mail. Rust (`mail_send.rs`) decodes and checks every file (a base64 that does not decode refuses the mail, and so does a total over 25 MB, before anything is asked of Gmail) and builds a `multipart/mixed` message: the first part is the text exactly as M-10 and M-11 build it (`text/plain`, or `multipart/alternative` when `markdown` is true), and each file follows as a part of `application/octet-stream` in base64 lines of 76 characters, with `Content-Disposition: attachment`. The name goes into the header only encoded: `filename="…"` holds the name with every character that is not printable ASCII (and every `"` and `\`) turned into `_`, and, when that changed the name, `filename*=UTF-8''…` (RFC 2231) holds the whole name percent-encoded, each parameter on a line of its own, so that no line break in a name can start a header or end the part. The boundaries are fixed texts (`tsunagiai-mixed` for the message, `tsunagiai-alternative` inside), which no base64 line can hold.

Why. The owner decided that files can be attached to a reply and to a new mail (2026-11-12; issue #486, and #491 for this part): from the clip beside "送信", or by a drop or a paste in the field, shown as chips under the text, with a ×.
