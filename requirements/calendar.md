# Calendar requirements

## C-1 View

Show the events of Google Calendar. Creating one is C-5; editing and deleting one are C-7.

The bar above the week (`#cal-bar`) is the tinted band of the header of the Todo screen: `--soft` with a 2px underline in `--accent`, at least `--header-h` high. It runs across the whole width of the screen, over the panel (C-4) and the week, which sit side by side under it. The bar holds, from the left, the button that opens and closes the panel (C-4), the button 「今日」 (shows the week that holds today), the arrows ‹ › (the week before and the week after), and the month of the week as a button, written as 「2026年11月」: the year and the month of the middle day of the week, run together, in one weight and one color. At the right end of the bar is the ✳ that opens and closes the Claude pane (C-6). The button of the panel is first in the bar whether the panel is open or closed, so nothing in the bar moves when the panel opens or closes. The month button opens the month calendar of C-4 (below). In the header of the days, today's date is a circle in `--accent`.

## C-2 Event detail

Press an event of the week (timed or all-day) and a popover beside it shows the color dot, the title, the time, a "Meet に参加" button with the URL and a copy button (only for a `https://meet.google.com/` link, from `hangoutLink`), the place, the description (HTML turned into text, links kept), the number of invited people (rooms left out) with how many accepted, the calendar and account, and the user's own answer. For an event that can be changed (C-7), a pencil (`aria-label` 「編集」) and a trash (「削除」) are in the head left of the ✕. Nothing is fetched on a press: the detail comes with the week and is kept with it in `calendar-cache.json` (the addresses of the invited, which the edit of C-7 starts its guests with, are fetched and kept too; no name of an attendee is). Another event switches it, the same one, ✕, Escape, a click elsewhere, a scroll, a resize, a week change or a screen change closes it, and a background redraw of the week keeps it open on the same event

## C-3 First day of the week

The settings screen has a "カレンダー" tab where the first day of the week is chosen from Sunday to Saturday (the default is Sunday). It is saved in `calendar-settings.json` in the app config directory and the calendar draws its weeks from that day

## C-4 Calendars to show

The calendar screen has a panel, 248px wide, left of the week and under the bar (C-1). The panel has no heading: the month calendar (below) is at its top, and the calendars are under it. The calendars are grouped by account. The heading of a group is a row of the account's avatar (the same initials and color as its avatar in the sidebar), its address and a chevron; pressing the row folds the account's calendars away and shows them again (`aria-expanded`; which accounts are folded is held in memory only, so every account is open when the app starts, and no file is added). Under the row the calendars are indented and held by a line (`--line`, 1px) at their left, and the space between accounts is clear. The calendars are in two parts, "マイカレンダー" (the calendars the account owns) and "他のカレンダー" (the others: a person's calendar added to the list, holidays, rooms), each as a checkbox and the calendar's name. The checkbox takes the calendar's color (the account's color when the calendar has none), and a part with no calendar is not drawn. Checking or unchecking a calendar saves the choice in `calendar-selection.json` in the app config directory, writes nothing to Google, and draws the week again from the new choice (once, 300 milliseconds after the last change of a run of them); the checkbox is disabled while its choice is being saved, and a choice that cannot be saved puts the checkbox back and shows the error. A saved change also deletes `calendar-cache.json`, so the weeks kept for the next start never hold a hidden calendar's events, and removing an account removes its choices from `calendar-selection.json`. A calendar never chosen follows `selected` at Google. The panel is read when the calendar is opened and again when the week is refreshed or the accounts change, and it is drawn again only when what was read differs from what is drawn. The button that closes and opens the panel (`#cal-bar-toggle`, `aria-label` 「カレンダー一覧」, `aria-expanded`) is the one button, first in the bar above the week (C-1): the panel has none of its own, and the button stays in the same place whether the panel is open or closed. Pressing it closes the panel and the week takes its width, and pressing it again opens the panel. The panel is open when the app starts, and whether it is closed is held in memory only

The month calendar. It is a small calendar of one month: its title 「2026年11月」 with ‹ › that move the month (`前の月`, `次の月`), the weekdays in a row, and the days in a row for each week. The weekdays start on the first day of the week of C-3. The row of the week shown is tinted (`--soft`), today is a circle in `--accent`, and the days of the months before and after are lighter. Pressing a day shows the week that holds it, through the same move as the arrows of the bar. It is at the top of the panel, outside the part that scrolls, and the month of the title of the bar opens the same calendar under the bar, over the week, so that a day can be chosen while the panel is closed too; pressing a day there moves to its week and closes it, and Escape, a press elsewhere and the month button again close it. The month shown follows the week drawn (the month of its middle day, as the title of the bar says) and is moved by the arrows of either calendar, which show the same month. The days are made by `calendar_month(year, month, firstDay)` of Rust (`month_grid` in `calendar.rs`: the weeks that hold the month, each of seven days from the first day of the week, answered with the weekday of each column), so that no date arithmetic is in the page, and nothing is saved.

## C-5 Create an event

An empty place of the week makes an event. A press on an empty place of a day's column (not on an event) draws a draft event of one hour that starts at the hour of the press (the hour lines of the week are the steps), and a press on an empty place of a day in the all-day band draws an all-day draft; the draft takes the color of the calendar chosen below. A popover opens beside the draft, of the frame of C-2 (400px wide), and a press on an event is C-2's as before.

The form, from the top: the title (`タイトルを追加`) beside the ✕; the start and the end, each as a date and a time, and the checkbox 「終日」 (checked, the times are hidden and the dates are the first and the last day); the guests (`ゲストを追加`: an address ended with Enter, a comma or leaving the field becomes a chip as the recipients of a mail do, × takes it off, an address that a chip already shows (the case does not count) is not added again, and no candidate is offered); the button 「Google Meet のビデオ会議を追加」, which becomes a card 「Google Meet」 with a × that takes it off (the URL is made when the event is saved); the description (`説明を追加`); the calendar, a select of the calendars the user owns (`calendar_owned`, which Rust answers with the calendars of `accessRole` `owner`; read the first time a form opens and kept until the accounts change; an answer with no calendar is not kept, so the next form reads again), and with more than one account each is written with its address; and 「保存」. The form starts on the primary calendar of the first account of the calendars shown (its ID is the account's address), else the first calendar of the list. The title, the times and the calendar show on the draft at once, and a change of the start moves the end by the same length. No place, repetition or notification is asked.

「保存」 calls `calendar_create_event` with the account and calendar, the title, the dates (all-day: `YYYY-MM-DD`, the end being the last day) or the times (RFC 3339 with this Mac's offset), the guests, whether Meet is wanted and the description. The button is disabled while it saves. When it succeeds the popover closes and the week's events are read again, which draws the event. When the answer is `null` the account has not granted the permission to create events, and the form says that it must log in again, with the button 「再ログイン」 (when the form was closed meanwhile, a card says it). When `calendar_owned` lists no calendar, the form says why in its error line: an account that must log in again, with the button 「再ログイン」, and an account whose calendars could not be read, with the error; 「保存」 stays disabled. When it fails the error is written in the form and what was written stays.

The edit of an event (C-7) is this form too, opened from the detail instead of from an empty place.

It closes with ✕, Escape, a press elsewhere, a week change and a screen change, as C-2 does, and the draft goes with it; what was written is lost and nothing asks first. A scroll or a resize of the week keeps it open, beside the draft. A redraw of the week keeps it and its draft.

## C-6 Claude pane on the calendar screen

The ✳ and the pane (the owner's decision of 2026-11-19, #1060 and #1062). The bar of C-1 has one more button, at its right end after the month (`#cal-bar-claude`, `.icon-btn`, `aria-label` "Claude に相談", the ✳ mark in `--claude`, `margin-left: auto`, `aria-pressed` false while the pane is closed and true while it is open, filled with `--accent` when pressed, as `#todo-tool-claude` of T-7). It opens and closes the pane. The pane is closed each time the app starts: whether it is open is held in memory only, so nothing is saved and no file is added. Unlike T-7 the closed pane is not drawn at all (no question and no button), and the week takes the whole width.

Where it is. Open, the pane `#cal-claude` (`aside.chat-pane`, `aria-label` "Claude") is at the right of the week, under the bar, in `#cal-frame` beside the panel of C-4 and the week, and the divider `#cal-split` (`.split-bar`, `aria-label` "Claude の幅") is between the week and the pane. The pane is 30% of the frame at first (`aria-valuenow` 30), widened or narrowed by the divider between 30% and 45% (`aria-valuemin` 30, `aria-valuemax` 45; the CSS holds the same range, with no least width in pixels; `ts/split.ts`), held in memory. The week takes what is left: the seven days stay in view, and closing the pane gives the week its width back. Opening or closing the pane closes an open event detail (C-2), as the button of the panel does (C-4). The heading of the pane is `.chat-head` of T-7, 48px high: the mark, the title "Claude" and "やり直す" (`#cal-claude-restart`). Under it is the terminal `#cal-claude-term` (`.claude-code-pane`). The screen shows no help text (`frontend.md`).

The pane. It is the Claude Code pane of A-36, with the pane id and the owner `calendar-claude` (`ts/calendar-claude.ts`, which calls `showClaudeCode`, `hideClaudeCode` and `closeClaudeCode` of `ts/claude-code.ts` as the Todo screen does, T-7). Claude Code gets the tools of `tsunagiai-mcp` only (`builtinTools` false) and no skill, and it runs in the `auto` permission mode with the calendar tools only (below). Pressing the ✳ while the pane is closed opens it: the process starts and the new terminal takes the keyboard. Pressing it while the pane is open closes it: the process ends with everything it started (`closeClaudeCode`, the one place that closes the pane), and the next open starts a new process. While the pane is closed, showing or leaving the screen and a change of the window's visibility start nothing. While it is open, the terminal is shown while the calendar screen is in view and the window is not hidden, and hidden otherwise; hiding leaves the process running, and showing it again starts nothing (A-36). "やり直す" ends the process with everything it started and starts a new one in the same terminal with the same first instruction; it is off until the start has answered. A process that ends by itself leaves the message of A-36 in the terminal and the pane stays open.

The permission mode (the owner's decision of 2026-11-19, #1070 and #1074).
`ts/calendar-claude.ts` starts the pane with `permissionMode` "auto", and it is the only pane that does (the other panes of A-36 start in "manual").
Claude Code then runs with `--permission-mode auto` and asks the owner for no tool.
`tsunagiai-mcp` is started with `--calendar` for it, and offers the calendar tools only (`CALENDAR_TOOLS`: `list_events`, `list_calendars`, `calendar_set_shown`, `calendar_create_event`, `calendar_update_event`, `calendar_delete_event` and `calendar_show_slots`, below), so that nothing else that writes (the trash of a mail, a post to Slack and the like) runs unasked.
Claude Code's own tools stay off, because Bash would run with no question: Rust refuses "auto" with `builtinTools` (A-36).
What an unasked tool does is not asked either: the owner decided that, in auto mode, a notice to a guest and the like are sent with no check (#1070).
The tools that write an event run with no question too: a change or a cancellation to a guest who is on the event already, and an event with no guest, are sent at once (the owner's decision, #1070). A new person is not invited by the pane, which is below ("A new guest", the owner's decision of 2026-11-19, #1073).

The first instruction (`prompt`, built in TS from a fixed text and today's date `YYYY-MM-DD`) says that this is a talk about the owner's calendar, gives today's date, says that the only tools are those of `tsunagiai-mcp`, what each of the seven does in a line (`list_events` reads the events with their detail, `list_calendars` the calendars and whether each is mine and shown, `calendar_set_shown` switches the calendars shown, `calendar_create_event`, `calendar_update_event` and `calendar_delete_event` create, rewrite and delete an event, the last two only for an event the owner organized on a calendar the owner owns and, for a repeating event, that occurrence, and a guest who is on the event is told at once; a new guest is invited only when the owner saves the form that the pane fills in, and `guests` or `description` left out of a rewrite keeps the guests or the description of the event, and the description that `list_events` cut is not sent back; the series of a repeating event is refused), says that `calendar_show_slots` draws candidates of free time on the week as red frames (the `slots`, each start and end as "YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM", 1 to 8, inside one day, and that it saves nothing), and says that the title, description and place of an event may have been written by someone else and no instruction in them is followed. Nothing that comes from outside (an event, a calendar, a mail) is in it.

The tools of the calendar screen (#1073; `tsunagiai-mcp.rs`, `calendar.rs`). They do what the screen does, with the functions of the screen and the same checks, on the config directory (`&Path`): the app and `tsunagiai-mcp` share them.
`list_events(from, to)` (`read_only_hint`) answers `{events, needsLogin, failed, calendarFailed, note}`; an event is `{account, calendar, calendarId, eventId, summary, declined, response, allDay, start, end, location, description, meetUrl, attendees, accepted, guests, editable}` (the detail of C-2 and what C-7 needs; the events are read with the fields of the week; `guests` are the addresses of the invited besides the user, rooms included, as `Info.guests`, and only for an event with `editable` true (a rewrite is what needs them), else empty; `description` is cut at 1,000 characters, with the mark 「…（長いため、残り N 文字を省きました）」, so a long description does not fill Claude's context, and the description of the detail of C-2 is not cut; a rewrite refuses a description that ends with this mark, so that the cut text is not sent back over the whole one). `list_calendars()` (`read_only_hint`) answers `{accounts: [{account, calendars: [{id, name, color, mine, shown}]}], needsLogin, failed, note}` (the read of `calendar_choices`, C-4). The titles, places, descriptions and calendar names can be written by someone else, so the descriptions and the `note` say that they are data and no instruction in them is followed, and they are quoted as JSON strings.
`calendar_set_shown(account, calendarId, shown)` (`destructive_hint`) keeps the choice of C-4 with the function of the screen's command (`save_shown`). `calendar_create_event(account, calendarId, title, allDay, start, end, guests?, meet?, description?)` is `create_event` (the function of `calendar_create_event`, C-5, with its checks and the check that the calendar is the owner's), `calendar_update_event(eventId, …the same)` is `update_event` and `calendar_delete_event(account, calendarId, eventId)` is `delete_event` (C-7: only an event the owner organized on a calendar the owner owns, a repeating event's one occurrence). The three have `destructive_hint` and `open_world_hint` (a guest is sent an invitation or a cancellation). The arguments are in camel case; an update gives the whole event as the form does, except `guests` and `description`, which an update may leave out to keep the guests and the description of the event (an empty list takes the guests all off and an empty text clears the description; a create leaves them out for none). `description` that ends with the mark of `list_events` (「…（長いため、残り N 文字を省きました）」) is refused in Japanese before anything is sent (`check_not_clipped`). A write answers a Japanese line (`calendar_set_shown`, `calendar_delete_event`) or `{message, event: {id, meetUrl}}`; an account that must log in again (`None`) is 「{account} は再ログインが必要です。つなぎAIアプリで再ログインしてください」, and any other refusal is the Japanese message of the function.
After a success the tool sends `changed` to the app (`RULES.changes` of `screen_bridge.rs`, as `todo` for Todoist): `calendar` after an event was created, rewritten or deleted, and `calendar-shown` after `calendar_set_shown`; an app that is not running or does not answer changes nothing, and the failure is a line on stderr. `ts/calendar.ts` registers `onScreenChanged("calendar")`: it drops the weeks drawn, fetched and kept, and reads the shown week again when the calendar screen is in view (else when the screen is shown), and it fetches no week ahead (the weeks before and after are read when they are shown); the calendars of the panel of C-4 are not read again, because an event does not change them. It registers `onScreenChanged("calendar-shown")` too, which does the same and also forgets the calendars of the panel and reads the weeks around the shown one ahead, because the calendars shown are what both hold. None of the tools but `calendar_show_slots` is offered with `--formation` (`FORMATION_KEPT_TOOLS` is unchanged).

A new guest (the owner's decision of 2026-11-19, plan C, #1073). The description of an event can be written by someone else, and a Claude in the auto mode that followed an instruction in it could send an invitation of the owner's event to an address that nobody chose. So the tools do not invite a new person: `calendar_create_event` with a guest, and `calendar_update_event` with `guests` that holds an address the event does not have (`events.get` `attendees`, the case of the address not counting), send nothing to Google. They check everything first (`create_event_for_claude` and `update_event_for_claude` of `calendar.rs`: the input, the calendar, and for an update the organizer, that the event is not the series of a repeating event and that the account is logged in; the pure function `has_new_guest` decides), and then ask the calendar screen, through the screen bridge, to open the form of C-5 (a new event) or of C-7 (the event) with the values that Claude gave (`Outcome::AskOwner`). The answer says that nothing is sent yet, that the form is filled in, and that the owner's 「保存」 sends it. The change or the cancellation of the guests that the event has, the guests left out of an update, and the create, the rewrite and the delete of an event with no guest, are sent at once as before. A delete is never held.
The target `calendar-form` of the screen bridge (`RULES.targets`; `ts/calendar-claude.ts` registers it, `writeForm` of `ts/calendar-create.ts` takes the write) is the way. It has two fields, `form` (JSON: `eventId` (empty for a new event), `account`, `calendarId`, `title`, `allDay`, `start`, `end`, `guests`, `meet`; `calendar::form_fields`) and `description` (the text, a field of its own because a field is at most 10,000 characters and the bridge takes 8 fields; left out when a rewrite leaves the description out, and then the form of an event starts with the description the event has, `Info.description`), and a read of it answers no field. It is open while the Claude pane is open and the calendar screen is shown, else the screen answers that it is not open, and the tool says in Japanese that nothing was sent (`form_refused`). A new event opens the form of C-5 beside its draft, with the calendar that Claude named selected (an account and calendar that the form's list of calendars does not have leaves the error 「指定されたカレンダーには予定を作れません」 in the form and 「保存」 disabled). An event of the shown week (the account, the calendar and the `eventId` are found among the events drawn now) opens the form of C-7 beside the event; an event that the week shown does not have is refused with 「その予定は、今カレンダーに出ている週にありません…」 and nothing is opened. A form already open is replaced. The write saves and sends nothing; 「保存」 is the form's own, which calls `calendar_create_event` or `calendar_update_event` of the screen with no hold, as C-5 and C-7 do.

Candidates of free time (#1063). The tool `calendar_show_slots` of `tsunagiai-mcp` (A-36) draws them: Claude Code reads the events (`list_events`), finds the free time and gives the candidates, 1 to 8, each a start and an end as "YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM" in this Mac's time. Rust checks them (`calendar::slot_fields`: the count, the shape, a time that exists, the end after the start, the same day) and gives the screen nothing when one is wrong; the error is Japanese and is the tool's result. The screen takes them as the target `calendar-slots` of the screen bridge (`ts/calendar-slots.ts`, registered in `ts/calendar-claude.ts`): the fields `slot1` to `slot8`, each `start/end`, a read answers the ones drawn now, and a write replaces them all (the tool's answer is 「画面に N 件の候補を描きました」). The target is open while the Claude pane is open, whichever screen is shown, so a call made while another screen is shown is taken and the frames are there when the calendar comes back; with the pane closed it answers that the screen is not open. They are kept in memory only: nothing is saved and nothing is sent, and the tool is `read_only_hint` (a hint only: a pane in the `manual` mode asked for it all the same, and the calendar pane asks for nothing because it is in the `auto` mode).

The frames. A candidate is a box in the column of its day, from its start to its end by the hour height (`.cal-slot`, in `.cal-col`, drawn again with each draw of the week, as the draft of C-5 is): a 2px dashed border in `--dot`, a faint `--dot` tint (7%), 4px corners, and at its top left the label 「候補{n} HH:MM–HH:MM」 (`.cal-slot-label`, the number from 1 in the order given, in `--dot`). A frame takes no press (`pointer-events: none`), so a press on the free place under it opens the form of C-5 as before. A candidate whose day is not in the shown week is not drawn, and is drawn when the week that holds it is shown (it stays in the list, and counts in the chip). The week's redraw, the screen change and a change of the calendars shown keep the frames.

The chip. While there is a candidate, the bar holds a chip (`#cal-slots-chip`, `.chip`) at the right of the month button and before the ✳: 「候補 N 件」 (`#cal-slots-count`, N counts every candidate, in the shown week or not) and a ✕ (`#cal-slots-clear`, `aria-label` 「候補を消す」), which clears them all: the frames and the chip go and nothing is asked. Closing the Claude pane (the ✳, one place: `closeCalendarClaude`) clears them too. With none, the chip is not drawn. There is no way back for a clear, and a write of Claude Code is not taken back with 「元に戻す」: the ✕ is the way.

Not here. What Anthropic is sent is Claude Code's own: the screen sends no calendar text of its own.

## C-7 Edit and delete an event

The owner's decision of 2026-11-19 (#1070). This requirement is the commands and the mark of Rust (#1071) and the screen that uses them (#1072); the tools of `tsunagiai-mcp` that use them (#1073) are in C-6.

The screen. The detail of C-2 of an event whose `editable` is true has a pencil (`aria-label` 「編集」) and a trash (「削除」), icon buttons (`.icon-btn`), in its head left of the ✕; an event whose `editable` is false (an invitation, or a calendar of someone else's, or a week kept by an older version) has neither. The screen shows no line that explains them.

The pencil closes the detail and opens the form of C-5 beside the event itself, as drawn by the last draw of the week (not by the one the detail was built with) (the event is marked as selected; there is no draft), with the values of the event: the title, the dates and times (an all-day event: the first and the last day, with 「終日」 checked), the guests as chips, the Meet as its card when the event has one, the description and the calendar. The values come with the week (`Info`: `allDay`, `start` and `end` as C-5 sends them, `guests`, `meetUrl` and `description`), and nothing is fetched. `guests` are the invited besides the user (a room is among them, so that a save keeps it); the description is the text as Google sends it. The calendar is shown with its account in a select that is disabled, so it cannot be changed, and no list of calendars is read. 「保存」 is enabled at once and calls `calendar_update_event` with the `eventId` and the fields as C-5 sends them (the account and the calendar are the event's own). It is disabled while it saves. When it succeeds the popover closes and the week's events are read again. When the answer is `null` the form says that the account must log in again to change events, with the button 「再ログイン」 (a card, when the form was closed meanwhile). When it fails the error is written in the form and what was written stays. ✕ and Escape close it with nothing changed and no command called, and so do the other ways that close the form of C-5; a scroll or a resize keeps it beside the event, and a redraw of the week puts it beside the event of the new draw, or closes it when the week no longer has the event.

The trash replaces the rows of the detail, under its head, with a confirmation in the same popover: 「「{title}」を削除します。元に戻せません。」, and, when the event has guests, a line 「ゲストに取り消しが届きます。」, with 「キャンセル」 and 「削除」 (red, as the confirmation of A-18). 「キャンセル」 puts the rows back and calls nothing. 「削除」 calls `calendar_delete_event` with the account, the calendar and the event; both buttons are disabled while it runs. When it succeeds the popover closes and the week's events are read again. When it fails the error is written in the confirmation and 「削除」 can be pressed again. When the answer is `null` the confirmation says that the account must log in again to change events, with the button 「再ログイン」. The trash in the head does nothing while the confirmation is shown. When the popover was closed before the answer, a failure and a login again go to a card (A-28), as a save does, and another event's detail that is open by then stays.

Which events. Only an event that the user organized (`organizer.self` of the event is true), on a calendar whose `accessRole` is `owner`, can be rewritten or deleted. An event the user was invited to, and an event on a calendar that someone else owns, are refused with a Japanese error. The decision is the pure function `can_change` of `calendar.rs`. Each event of the week carries its result, `editable`, with its `eventId` and `calendarId`, in the data that C-2 draws (`Info`), so that the screen offers a change only for an event that can be changed. A week kept by an older version has none of the three, and its events are not `editable` until the week is read again. The commands do not trust the screen: each reads the calendar's role (`check_owned`, as C-5 does) and the event (`events.get`: `organizer(self)`, `hangoutLink`, `recurrence` and `attendees`) from Google, and refuses what the mark would not have allowed.

A repeating event. The week is read with `singleEvents`, so `eventId` is the ID of one occurrence, and a change or a delete reaches that occurrence only. The series ("all events", "this and the following") is not handled, and the event that has `recurrence` (the series itself, whose ID is not one occurrence's) is refused with a Japanese error by both commands (`check_not_series`, in `check_current` after the organizer), so that a tool that gives its ID cannot change or delete every occurrence.

`calendar_update_event(event)`. `event` is the form of C-5 (`NewEvent`: the account and the calendar, the title, the dates or the times, the guests, whether Meet is wanted, the description) with the `eventId`. It is checked as C-5 checks it (the length of the title and the description, the dates and times, the guests), and then sent as `events.patch` of that event. Each field of the form is named in the body, an empty title, an empty description and an empty list of guests too, so that the form can clear them; the guests are the whole list after the change: a guest who stays is sent as `events.get` gave the entry (so `optional`, `displayName` and the answer are kept), a new address is `{"email"}`. A rewrite of a tool of Claude may leave the guests out (C-6): the body then names no `attendees`, and the guests of the event stay; it may leave the description out in the same way: the body then names no `description`, and the description stays. The form of the time that is not used (`dateTime` for an all-day event, `date` for a timed one) is sent as `null`, so an all-day event can become a timed one and back. The Meet is made (`conferenceData.createRequest`, `conferenceDataVersion=1`) when it is wanted and the event has none, taken off (`conferenceData` `null`) when it is not wanted and the event has one, and left as it is otherwise. `sendUpdates` is `all` when there are guests after the change or there were before it (so that a guest who was taken off is told too), else `none`. It answers as `calendar_create_event` does (`CreatedEvent`: the ID and the Meet link).

`calendar_delete_event(account, calendarId, eventId)`. It sends `events.delete` for that event, with `sendUpdates` `all` when the event has guests, else `none`, and answers `true`.

The answer `null` of both means that the account has not granted the permission to change events (a 403 for the scope, or a token that can no longer be refreshed): the screen sends the user to log in again, as C-5 does. Any other failure is its Japanese message. Both are written as functions of the config directory (`update_event` and `delete_event`, which take `&Path`, the `Auth` and the `CalendarCache`), so that `tsunagiai-mcp` can call them. After a change or a delete the screen reads the week again, as after C-5; the commands keep nothing besides what C-5 keeps (they forget the account's list of calendars when it must log in again).
